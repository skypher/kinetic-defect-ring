\begin{abstract}
We derive exact characteristic equations for the spectrum of a
continuous-time run-and-tumble particle
on an $n$-site ring whose tumbling rate is $\omega_0$ at one site and
$\omega$ elsewhere.  The defect is a rank-one perturbation in the
polarization channel.  The resulting derivative identity and parity
factorization separate a protected sector from an affected scalar Green
equation and determine the finite-ring Jordan structure, including the
singular flat band at $\omega=1$.  On the infinite line, the same Green
function characterizes every off-bulk localized mode, while a transfer
argument excludes embedded $\ell^2$ modes away from the explicitly resolved
flat band.  For fixed $\omega>0$, reflection protection prevents a slow
defect---even one with $\omega_0\asymp n^{-1}$---from changing the leading
$n^{-2}$ gap.  When $\omega=\alpha/n$ and $\omega_0=\beta/n$, the gap is
instead of order $n^{-1}$, and its controlling parity changes across the
limiting first-mode exceptional scale $\alpha=2\pi$.  The standard telegraph
model omits a componentwise lattice-diffusion term generated by Poisson
hopping; restoring it recovers the leading fixed-rate lattice decay, with a
dispersion discrepancy of order $n^{-4}$.
\end{abstract}

\vskip5pt
\noindent\textbf{Keywords.}\enspace Run-and-tumble particle; kinetic

\section{Introduction}

Persistent random walks describe motion with finite directional memory.
The connection between correlated directional motion and the telegraph
equation goes back to \cite[abstract]{Goldstein1951}.  Continuum formulations
of biased chemotactic motion arise, for example, in \cite{Schnitzer1993},
while lattice run-and-tumble models retain the
individual hopping and reversal events and connect directly to
nonequilibrium particle systems
\cite{ThompsonTailleurCatesBlythe2011}.  For a homogeneous ring, the
one-particle Fourier blocks used here coincide with the
relative-coordinate blocks extracted in the two-particle analysis of
\cite{MallminBlytheEvans2019}; that work gives their spectrum,
exceptional points, and associated spectral-decay transition explicitly.
For a continuum relative-coordinate model with thermal diffusion,
\cite[Secs.~3.2--3.3]{DasDharKundu2020} analyzes relaxation, four
symmetry sectors, and the low-lying spectrum on a ring.
Section~\ref{sec:model} records the precise convention used below.

Spatial inhomogeneity has been studied through position-dependent speed
\cite{AngelaniGarra2019}, quenched disorder in speed and tumbling
\cite[Secs.~II.A.2--II.A.3 and II.B.2--II.B.3]
{BenDorWoillezKafriKardarSolon2019}, saturating space-dependent rates
\cite[Sec.~II, Eqs.~(3)--(4)]{JainChatterjee2024}, and direction- and
position-dependent reversal rates
\cite[Secs.~2--4]{SinghSabhapanditKundu2020}.  These works emphasize
probability laws, steady states, first-passage behavior, or relaxation in
extended media.  Impurity-activated reversals and moving tumble sites
also occur in interacting periodic exclusion processes
\cite[Secs.~V--VII]{ChatterjeeHayakawa2023},
\cite[Secs.~II--IV]{DasChatterjee2025}; their focus is driven clustering
and many-particle steady behavior rather than the full one-particle
spectrum of a fixed tumbling-rate defect.

Related lattice-impurity methods include persistent walks with random
asymmetric transmittances \cite{SadjadiMiri2008}, Chebyshev propagators
and partially absorbing defects for confined nonpersistent walks
\cite[Secs.~VI--VII and Apps.~C and E]{Giuggioli2020}, and the
difference-equation construction of isolated impurity levels
\cite[abstract]{KosterSlater1954}.  Green-function reduction of a point
perturbation has also been used for non-Hermitian lattices
\cite[Secs.~II--III and Eqs.~(11)--(15)]{Roccati2021}.  Finite-size
impurity spectra and impurity-induced size-dependent localization have
been studied in nonreciprocal chains \cite{LiuZengLiChen2021}.
Complex-impurity localization and exceptional points in otherwise
Hermitian reciprocal lattices are treated in
\cite{KokkinakisKomisMakrisEconomou2026}.  Those tight-binding problems
concern on-site potentials, boundary sensitivity, and skin or bound-state
localization.  Here the local perturbation acts instead on the tumbling
channel of a stochastic generator.

Rank-one determinant and resolvent identities are the finite-dimensional
mechanism behind the scalar reduction; an early inverse-update formula appears
in \cite[pp.~124--127]{ShermanMorrison1950}.  For scalar second-order difference
operators, transfer matrices, Green kernels, and periodic monodromy are
developed in \cite[Secs.~1.1--1.2 and 7.1]{Teschl2000}.  The operator here is
instead a two-component nonnormal stochastic generator, so the parity and
Jordan analysis is carried out directly.  Sections~14--15 of
\cite{TrefethenEmbree2005} review the distinction between asymptotic spectral
decay and transient behavior for nonnormal matrix exponentials.

\section{Model and notation}\label{sec:model}

\begin{definition}[Defective run-and-tumble generator]\label{def:generator}
Let $n\geq2$, let $\omega>0$, and let $\omega _0\geq0$.  Sites are
$j\in\Z/n\Z$, orientations are $\sigma\in\{+,-\}$, and
\[
  \omega_j=\begin{cases}\omega_0,&j=0,\\ \omega,&j\ne0.\end{cases}
\]
The forward generator $L=L_n(\omega,\omega_0)$ is
\begin{align}
 (Lu)^+_j&=u^+_{j-1}-(1+\omega_j)u^+_j+\omega_j u^-_j,\label{eq:generator-plus}\\
 (Lu)^-_j&=u^-_{j+1}-(1+\omega_j)u^-_j+\omega_j u^+_j.\label{eq:generator-minus}
\end{align}
Thus hopping in the current orientation has rate one and tumbling has
rate $\omega_j$.  We regard $u=(u_j^+,u_j^-)_{j\in\Z/n\Z}$ as a column
vector in $\C^{2n}$ and write $e_{j,\sigma}$ for the corresponding
coordinate vector.  With this convention, the columns of $L$ sum to zero,
so $\partial_tu=Lu$ preserves the total mass of a probability vector.  Put
\[
 \delta=\omega_0-\omega,\qquad
 q_k=\frac{2\pi k}{n},\qquad c_k=\cos q_k,\qquad s_k=\sin q_k.
\]
\end{definition}

Equations \eqref{eq:generator-plus}--\eqref{eq:generator-minus} use the
same one-particle convention as equations (6)--(10) of
\cite{MallminBlytheEvans2019}.  That paper records the homogeneous
eigenvalues in its equation (10), the homogeneous exceptional points in

\begin{lemma}[Rank-one defect]\label{lem:rank-one}
Let $L^{(0)}=L_n(\omega,\omega)$.  If
$d=e_{0,+}-e_{0,-}$, then
\[
 L=L^{(0)}-\delta\,dd^{\mathsf T}.
\]
In particular, the perturbation has rank one when $\delta\ne0$.
Here ${}^{\mathsf T}$ is the matrix transpose in the displayed coordinate
basis; Hermitian inner products are written with angle brackets below.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At site zero, changing $\omega$ to $\omega_0$ adds
\[
 \delta\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}
 =-\delta\begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\end{pmatrix}
             \begin{pmatrix}1&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Every other matrix entry is unchanged.
\end{proof}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Notation guide.}
The scalar $\delta$ is the defect contrast and $d$ is the polarization
vector at the defect; distances are always written explicitly below.
The unadorned $R$ denotes kinetic reflection, whereas decorated symbols
such as $R_n$ and $R_{\ell,n}^{\pm}$ denote pole residues.  The finite and
infinite polarization Green functions are $G_n$ and $g_\infty$,
respectively.  In the gap sections, $q=2\pi/n$ is the first finite-ring
wave number and $q_\star=2\pi$ is its scaled value.

\section{Equivalent characteristic formulations}\label{sec:characteristic}

\begin{definition}[Homogeneous factors]\label{def:homogeneous-factors}
For $\lambda\in\C$, set
\begin{align}
 a&=\lambda+1+\omega,\label{eq:a-def}\\
 x&=\frac{a^2+1-\omega^2}{2a},\label{eq:x-def}\\
 D_k(\lambda)&=(\lambda+1-c_k)(\lambda+1+2\omega-c_k)+s_k^2
             =a^2+1-\omega^2-2ac_k.\label{eq:Dk}
\end{align}
Although $x$ is undefined at $a=0$, each expression below containing
$a^nT_n(x)$ is understood by its unique polynomial continuation.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Defect-free characteristic polynomial]
\label{lem:homogeneous-polynomial}
The defect-free characteristic polynomial is
\begin{align}
 P_n^{(0)}(\lambda;\omega)
  &=\det(\lambda I-L^{(0)})\notag\\
  &=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}D_k(\lambda)\label{eq:P0-product}\\
  &=2a^n\bigl(T_n(x)-1\bigr),\label{eq:P0-cheb}
\end{align}
where $T_n$ is the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind.
The two roots of $D_k$ are
\[
 \lambda_k^\pm=c_k-1-\omega
       \pm\sqrt{\omega^2-s_k^2}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fourier transformation gives the block
\[
 W_k=\begin{pmatrix}e^{\ii q_k}-1-\omega&\omega\\
                     \omega&e^{-\ii q_k}-1-\omega\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its characteristic polynomial is $D_k$, which proves
\eqref{eq:P0-product} and the displayed roots.  Since
$D_k=2a(x-c_k)$ and
\[
 \prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(x-c_k)=2^{1-n}\bigl(T_n(x)-1\bigr),
\]
equation \eqref{eq:P0-cheb} follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact finite-ring characteristic polynomial]\label{thm:characteristic}
For every $n\geq2$, $\omega>0$, and $\omega_0\geq0$,
\begin{align}
 P_n(\lambda;\omega,\omega_0)
  &:=\det(\lambda I-L_n(\omega,\omega_0))\label{eq:P-def}\\
  &=P_n^{(0)}(\lambda;\omega)
    +\frac{\delta}{n}\,\partial_\omega
       P_n^{(0)}(\lambda;\omega)\label{eq:derivative-form}\\
  &=P_n^{(0)}(\lambda;\omega)
    \left[1+\frac{2\delta}{n}
      \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{\lambda+1-c_k}{D_k(\lambda)}\right].
      \label{eq:resolvent-form}
\end{align}
Equation \eqref{eq:resolvent-form} is interpreted by polynomial
continuation at a homogeneous eigenvalue.  Equivalently,
\begin{align}
 P_n={}&2a^n(T_n(x)-1)
      +2\delta a^{n-1}(T_n(x)-1)
      +\delta a^{n-2}\lambda(\lambda+2)U_{n-1}(x),
      \label{eq:cheb-defect}
\end{align}
where $U_{n-1}$ is the Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind and the
right-hand side again denotes its polynomial continuation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $B=\lambda I-L^{(0)}$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:rank-one} and the matrix
determinant lemma give
\[
 \det(\lambda I-L)=\det B\bigl(1+\delta d^{\mathsf T}B^{-1}d\bigr).
\]
In Fourier space,
\[
 d^{\mathsf T}B^{-1}d
   =\frac1n\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}
      \frac{2(\lambda+1-c_k)}{D_k(\lambda)},
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:resolvent-form}.  Direct differentiation of
\eqref{eq:P0-product} gives
\[
 \partial_\omega P_n^{(0)}
 =P_n^{(0)}\sum_k\frac{2(\lambda+1-c_k)}{D_k},
\]
and hence \eqref{eq:derivative-form}.  Finally,
\[
 \partial_\omega x=\frac{\lambda(\lambda+2)}{2a^2},
 \qquad T_n'(x)=nU_{n-1}(x),
\]
and differentiating \eqref{eq:P0-cheb} proves
\eqref{eq:cheb-defect}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Transfer-matrix quantization]\label{thm:transfer}
For a local rate $r\geq0$, define
\begin{equation}
 T_r(\lambda)=\frac1{\lambda+1+r}
 \begin{pmatrix}
  1&r\\
  -r&(\lambda+1+r)^2-r^2
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:transfer}
\end{equation}
Then $\det T_r=1$, and, away from the displayed denominators,
\begin{equation}
 P_n(\lambda;\omega,\omega_0)
 =a^{n-1}(\lambda+1+\omega_0)
   \left\{\operatorname{tr}
     \bigl[T_\omega(\lambda)^{n-1}T_{\omega_0}(\lambda)\bigr]-2\right\}.
 \label{eq:transfer-char}
\end{equation}
After multiplication by the denominators, this identity holds for every
$\lambda$ by polynomial continuation.  When $a\ne0$ and
$\lambda+1+\omega_0\ne0$, an eigenvalue is equivalently characterized by
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{tr}
     \bigl[T_\omega(\lambda)^{n-1}T_{\omega_0}(\lambda)\bigr]=2.
 \label{eq:trace-two}
\end{equation}
At a zero of either denominator, the cleared polynomial identity
\eqref{eq:transfer-char}, rather than the undefined trace in
\eqref{eq:trace-two}, is the eigenvalue criterion.
Moreover, if $t=\operatorname{tr}T_\omega=2x$, then
\begin{equation}
 T_\omega^{n-1}=U_{n-2}(x)T_\omega-U_{n-3}(x)I,
 \label{eq:transfer-cheb}
\end{equation}
with $U_{-1}=0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The eigenvalue equations at site $j$ are
\begin{align*}
 (\lambda+1+\omega_j)u_j^+-\omega_j u_j^-&=u_{j-1}^+,\\
 (\lambda+1+\omega_j)u_j^--\omega_j u_j^+&=u_{j+1}^-.
\end{align*}
Consequently
\[
 \binom{u_j^+}{u_{j+1}^-}
 =T_{\omega_j}(\lambda)\binom{u_{j-1}^+}{u_j^-}.
\]
Periodic boundary conditions require the product monodromy to have
eigenvalue one.  Its determinant is one, so this is equivalent to trace
two.  To identify the polynomial, retain temporarily arbitrary rates
$r_0,\ldots,r_{n-1}$, denote the resulting generator by
$L_n(r_0,\ldots,r_{n-1})$, and put $b_j=\lambda+1+r_j$.
Let $E_j^+$ and $E_j^-$ denote the two equation residuals, with their
right-hand sides moved to the left.  For $b_j\ne0$, replace each row pair
by
\[
 \binom{F_j^+}{F_j^-}
 =\begin{pmatrix}b_j^{-1}&0\\-r_jb_j^{-1}&-1\end{pmatrix}
   \binom{E_j^+}{E_j^-}.
\]
This block row operation has determinant $-b_j^{-1}$.  If
$y_j=(u_j^+,u_{j+1}^-)^{\mathsf T}$, the transformed equations are
exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transfer-cyclic-system}
 y_j-T_{r_j}(\lambda)y_{j-1}=0,
 \qquad j\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z.
\end{equation}
Let $A$ be the original coefficient matrix, $F$ the matrix after these
row operations, and $C$ the matrix of
\eqref{eq:transfer-cyclic-system} with columns ordered as
$y_0,y_1,\ldots,y_{n-1}$.  The column reordering cyclically sends
$(u_0^-,u_1^-,\ldots,u_{n-1}^-)$ to
$(u_1^-,\ldots,u_{n-1}^-,u_0^-)$ and fixes the plus variables, so its
sign is $(-1)^{n-1}$.  Therefore
\[
 \det F=\frac{(-1)^n}{\prod_jb_j}\det A,
 \qquad \det C=(-1)^{n-1}\det F.
\]
Successively eliminating $y_0,\ldots,y_{n-2}$ in
\eqref{eq:transfer-cyclic-system}, using its identity diagonal blocks,
leaves
$(I-M)y_{n-1}=0$, where $M=T_{r_{n-1}}\cdots T_{r_0}$; hence
$\det C=\det(I-M)$.  Combining the three determinant identities gives
\[
 \det A=-\prod_jb_j\det(I-M)=-\prod_jb_j\det(M-I).
\]
Since $\det M=1$, one has
$-\det(M-I)=\operatorname{tr}M-2$.  Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-transfer-determinant}
 \det(\lambda I-L_n(r_0,\ldots,r_{n-1}))
 =\left(\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}b_j\right)
  \left[\operatorname{tr}(T_{r_{n-1}}\cdots T_{r_0})-2\right].
\end{equation}
This is an identity of rational functions where all $b_j\ne0$.  The
displayed product clears every denominator on the right, giving a
polynomial, so the identity extends to every $\lambda$.  Taking
$r_0=\omega_0$ and all other rates equal to $\omega$, and using cyclicity
of the trace, gives
\eqref{eq:transfer-char}, including algebraic multiplicities.  Equation
\eqref{eq:transfer-cheb} is the Cayley--Hamilton identity for a
unimodular $2\times2$ matrix.
\end{proof}

\section{Parity factorization and finite-ring exceptional points}
\label{sec:parity}

\subsection{Reflection and parity polynomials}

\begin{definition}[Kinetic reflection]\label{def:reflection}
Define the involution
\[
 (Ru)^+_j=u^-_{-j},\qquad (Ru)^-_j=u^+_{-j}.
\]
Write $\mathcal H_\pm=\ker(R\mp I)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Protected sector]\label{lem:protected-sector}
The generator commutes with $R$, the defect vector satisfies $Rd=-d$,
and
\[
 L|_{\mathcal H_+}=L^{(0)}|_{\mathcal H_+}.
\]
Thus the defect changes only the reflection-odd sector.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Reflection reverses position and orientation simultaneously, leaving the
directed hopping rules and the rate field $\omega_j$ invariant.  At the
fixed site zero it exchanges the two entries of $d$, hence $Rd=-d$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:rank-one} then vanishes on $\mathcal H_+$.
\end{proof}

We call $\mathcal H_+$ the protected sector and $\mathcal H_-$ the
affected sector.  These terms refer only to the response to the defect:
both sectors retain their full homogeneous spectral dependence on
$\omega$.

\begin{definition}[Parity polynomials]\label{def:parity-polynomials}
Put $m=\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor$ and let $\varepsilon_n$ be one for even
$n$ and zero for odd $n$.  Define
\begin{align}
 H_{n,+}(\lambda)
   &=\lambda(\lambda+2)^{\varepsilon_n}
     \prod_{k=1}^{m}D_k(\lambda),\label{eq:Hplus}\\
 H_{n,-}^{(0)}(\lambda)
   &=(\lambda+2\omega)
     (\lambda+2+2\omega)^{\varepsilon_n}
     \prod_{k=1}^{m}D_k(\lambda),\label{eq:Hminus0}\\
 G_n(\lambda)
   &=\frac1n\left[
       \frac1{\lambda+2\omega}
       +\frac{\varepsilon_n}{\lambda+2+2\omega}
       +2\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\lambda+1-c_k}{D_k(\lambda)}
     \right],\label{eq:Gfinite}\\
 Q_{n,-}(\lambda)
   &=H_{n,-}^{(0)}(\lambda)\bigl(1+2\delta G_n(\lambda)\bigr).
   \label{eq:Qminus}
\end{align}
Each displayed denominator in \eqref{eq:Qminus} divides
$H_{n,-}^{(0)}$, so $Q_{n,-}$ is a monic polynomial of degree $n$.
An entirely polynomial version is
\begin{align}
Q_{n,-}={}&H_{n,-}^{(0)}+\frac{2\delta}{n}\left[
 \frac{H_{n,-}^{(0)}}{\lambda+2\omega}
 +\varepsilon_n\frac{H_{n,-}^{(0)}}{\lambda+2+2\omega}\right.\notag\\
&\left.\hspace{38mm}
 +2\sum_{k=1}^{m}(\lambda+1-c_k)
       \frac{H_{n,-}^{(0)}}{D_k}\right].\label{eq:Qminus-poly}
\end{align}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Exact parity factorization]\label{thm:parity-factor}
The characteristic polynomial factors as
\begin{equation}
 P_n(\lambda;\omega,\omega_0)
   =H_{n,+}(\lambda)Q_{n,-}(\lambda).
 \label{eq:parity-factor}
\end{equation}
The first factor is the characteristic polynomial on $\mathcal H_+$;
the second is the characteristic polynomial on $\mathcal H_-$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The modes $q_k$ and $-q_k$ form a four-dimensional Fourier subspace,
which splits into two isomorphic two-dimensional $R$-eigenspaces.  Each
has characteristic polynomial $D_k$.  At $q=0$, the vectors $(1,1)$ and
$(1,-1)$ have parities $+$ and $-$ and eigenvalues $0$ and $-2\omega$.
When $n$ is even, the $q=\pi$ density and polarization vectors have
parities $+$ and $-$ and eigenvalues $-2$ and $-2-2\omega$.  This gives
\eqref{eq:Hplus}--\eqref{eq:Hminus0}.
Lemma~\ref{lem:protected-sector} and the rank-one determinant lemma restricted
to $\mathcal H_-$ give \eqref{eq:Qminus}.  Multiplication proves
\eqref{eq:parity-factor}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Jordan structure and the exceptional locus}

\begin{definition}[Exceptional point]\label{def:exceptional}
For an eigenvalue $\mu$, let $m_{\rm alg}(\mu)$ be its algebraic
multiplicity and
$m_{\rm geo}(\mu)=\dim\ker(\mu I-L)$ its geometric multiplicity.  We call
$(\omega,\omega_0,\mu)$ an exceptional point when
$m_{\rm alg}(\mu)>m_{\rm geo}(\mu)$.
Write $J_r(\mu)$ for a Jordan block of size $r$ at $\mu$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[No inter-wave coincidences away from the flat band]
\label{lem:no-coincidences}
If $c_k\ne c_\ell$ and $D_k(\mu)=D_\ell(\mu)=0$, then $\omega=1$ and
$\mu=-2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Subtracting the two equations gives
$2a(c_k-c_\ell)=0$, hence $a=0$.  Substitution into
\eqref{eq:Dk} gives $1-\omega^2=0$.  Since $\omega>0$, this means
$\omega=1$ and then $\mu=-1-\omega=-2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Scalar resolvent criterion for cyclicity]
\label{lem:scalar-resolvent-cyclicity}
Let $A\in\mathbb C^{N\times N}$ and $v\in\mathbb C^N$.  Suppose the
reduced denominator of the scalar rational function
\[
 v^{\mathsf T}(\lambda I-A)^{-1}v
\]
is the characteristic polynomial $\chi_A(\lambda)$.  Then $v$ is a
cyclic vector for $A$.  Moreover, for every $\eta\in\mathbb C$, the
same vector is cyclic for $A-\eta vv^{\mathsf T}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $m_{A,v}$ be the monic polynomial of least degree satisfying
$m_{A,v}(A)v=0$.  For $\lambda\notin\spec A$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vector-minimal-resolvent}
 m_{A,v}(\lambda)(\lambda I-A)^{-1}v
 =\frac{m_{A,v}(\lambda)I-m_{A,v}(A)}{\lambda I-A}\,v.
\end{equation}
The matrix quotient on the right is a matrix polynomial in $\lambda$:
for each monomial it is the finite divided-difference identity
\[
 \frac{\lambda^jI-A^j}{\lambda I-A}
   =\sum_{r=0}^{j-1}\lambda^{j-1-r}A^r.
\]
Thus the reduced denominator of every scalar component of
$(\lambda I-A)^{-1}v$, and in particular of the displayed scalar
resolvent, divides $m_{A,v}$.  By hypothesis, $\chi_A$ therefore
divides $m_{A,v}$.  Conversely, Cayley--Hamilton gives
$\chi_A(A)v=0$.  Write $\chi_A=q\,m_{A,v}+r$ with
$\deg r<\deg m_{A,v}$.  Then $r(A)v=0$, so the defining minimality of
$m_{A,v}$ forces $r=0$.  Hence $m_{A,v}$ divides $\chi_A$, and therefore
$m_{A,v}=\chi_A$ and has degree $N$, which is equivalent to
$v,Av,\ldots,A^{N-1}v$ spanning $\mathbb C^N$.

Put $B=A-\eta vv^{\mathsf T}$.  Induction on $j$ gives
\[
 B^jv=A^jv+\sum_{r=0}^{j-1}c_{j,r}A^rv
\]
for suitable scalars $c_{j,r}$.  The change from the $A$-Krylov list
to the $B$-Krylov list is therefore triangular with diagonal entries
one.  The two lists have the same span, so $v$ is cyclic for $B$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Jordan classification for $\omega\ne1$]
\label{thm:jordan-regular}
Assume $\omega\ne1$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item In the protected sector, the endpoint factors in
  \eqref{eq:Hplus} give one-dimensional blocks.  For each
  $1\leq k\leq m$, a root of $D_k$ gives one $J_1$, except that
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:protected-ep}
    \omega=|s_k|,\qquad
    \mu=c_k-1-\omega.
  \end{equation}
  gives one $J_2(\mu)$.
\item In the affected sector, if
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:affected-ep}
    Q_{n,-}(\mu)=Q_{n,-}'(\mu)=\cdots
      =Q_{n,-}^{(r-1)}(\mu)=0,\qquad
    Q_{n,-}^{(r)}(\mu)\ne0,
  \end{equation}
  then the complete affected-sector contribution at $\mu$ is one
  block $J_r(\mu)$.
\item The full Jordan form at $\mu$ is the direct sum of the blocks in
  items 1 and 2.  In particular, a crossing between the two parity
  sectors is semisimple unless one of its sector blocks has size greater
  than one.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, \eqref{eq:protected-ep} together with
\eqref{eq:affected-ep} is an exact finite algebraic criterion for every
exceptional point when $\omega\ne1$.  The order $r$ in \eqref{eq:affected-ep}
classifies exceptional points of arbitrary order.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The discriminant of $D_k$ is $4(\omega^2-s_k^2)$.  At a zero
discriminant its Fourier block is not scalar and hence is a single
$J_2$; otherwise each root is simple.
Lemma~\ref{lem:no-coincidences} excludes coincidences between distinct
$c_k$ values.

It remains to show that the affected restriction is cyclic.  In each
two-dimensional homogeneous parity block, the spectral weight of the
defect vector has numerator $2(\lambda+1-c_k)$.  This numerator and
$D_k$ have no common root for an interior wave number: a common root
would give $\lambda+1=c_k$, after which $D_k=s_k^2\ne0$.  The endpoint
weights in \eqref{eq:Gfinite} are also nonzero.  At a double root of
an interior $D_k$, the numerator remains nonzero, so the scalar
resolvent retains a pole of order two.  Regard the endpoint factors as
the odd-parity roots of $D_0$ and, when $n$ is even, $D_{n/2}$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:no-coincidences} then makes their root sets disjoint from
those of every interior factor and from one another when $\omega\ne1$.
Consequently no pole, including a repeated pole, cancels when the block
fractions are put over their common denominator.  The scalar resolvent
$d^{\mathsf T}(\lambda I-L^{(0)}|_{\mathcal H_-})^{-1}d=2G_n(\lambda)$
therefore has reduced denominator $H_{n,-}^{(0)}$, the characteristic
polynomial of the homogeneous odd restriction.
Lemma~\ref{lem:scalar-resolvent-cyclicity} makes $d$ cyclic first for that
restriction and then for its update by $-\delta dd^{\mathsf T}$.  Hence
the minimal and characteristic polynomials of the affected restriction
both equal $Q_{n,-}$.  A cyclic matrix has one Jordan block for each
eigenvalue, of size equal to the root order.  The direct-sum assertion
follows from the invariant parity splitting.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Flat-band classification at $\omega=1$]
\label{thm:flat-band}
Let $\omega=1$ and put $\mu=-2$.
Its Jordan contribution is given by the following table, where
$J_r(\mu)^{\oplus a}$ denotes the direct sum of $a$ copies.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{L{0.57\textwidth}L{0.31\textwidth}}
\hline
Parameters & Contribution at $\mu$\\
\hline
$\omega_0=1$ and $4\nmid n$
  & $J_1(\mu)^{\oplus n}$\\
$\omega_0=1$ and $4\mid n$
  & $J_2(\mu)^{\oplus2}\oplus J_1(\mu)^{\oplus(n-2)}$\\
$\omega_0\ne1$ and $n\equiv1,2\pmod4$
  & $J_1(\mu)^{\oplus(n-1)}$\\
$\omega_0\ne1$ and $n\equiv0\pmod4$
  & $J_2(\mu)\oplus J_1(\mu)^{\oplus(n-2)}$\\
$n\equiv3\pmod4$, $\omega_0\ne1$, and
$\omega_0\ne(n-1)/(n+1)$
  & $J_2(\mu)\oplus J_1(\mu)^{\oplus(n-2)}$\\
$n\equiv3\pmod4$ and $\omega_0=(n-1)/(n+1)$
  & $J_3(\mu)\oplus J_1(\mu)^{\oplus(n-2)}$\\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For eigenvalues $\lambda\ne-2$, every protected-sector root is a
one-dimensional block.  Remove the exact power of $(\lambda+2)$
specified above from $Q_{n,-}$.  Every root of the remaining affected
polynomial contributes one Jordan block, so the derivative test
\eqref{eq:affected-ep} applied to that reduced polynomial classifies
every remaining affected block at $\omega=1$.  The full
Jordan form is the direct sum of the protected and affected blocks.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Algebraic multiplicity at the flat point.}
Write $t=\lambda+2$.  At $\omega=1$,
\[
 D_k=t(t-2c_k),\qquad
 P_n=t^{n-1}F_n(t),
\]
where, for $\delta=\omega_0-1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flat-F}
 F_n(t)=2t\bigl(T_n(t/2)-1\bigr)
 +\delta\left[2\bigl(T_n(t/2)-1\bigr)
 +(t-2)U_{n-1}(t/2)\right].
\end{equation}
Put $C_n=\cos(n\pi/2)$ and $S_n=\sin(n\pi/2)$.  The values
$T_n(0)=C_n$, $U_{n-1}(0)=S_n$, together with
\[
 T_n'(0)=nS_n,\qquad U_{n-1}'(0)=-nC_n,
\]
give directly from \eqref{eq:flat-F}
\begin{align}
 F_n(0)&=2\delta(C_n-1-S_n),\label{eq:flat-value}\\
 F_n'(0)&=2(C_n-1)
   +\delta\bigl((n+1)S_n+nC_n\bigr).
   \label{eq:flat-first-derivative}
\end{align}
If $\delta=0$ and $4\nmid n$, the derivative in
\eqref{eq:flat-first-derivative} is nonzero.  If $\delta=0$ and
$4\mid n$, the Chebyshev differential equation at zero gives
$T_n''(0)=-n^2$, and hence
\[
 F_n(t)=-\frac{n^2}{4}t^3+O(t^5).
\]
For $\delta\ne0$, equations
\eqref{eq:flat-value}--\eqref{eq:flat-first-derivative} give
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 n\pmod4 & \text{first nonzero datum} & \operatorname{ord}_{t=0}F_n\\
\hline
1,2 & F_n(0)=-4\delta &0\\
0   & F_n'(0)=n\delta &1\\
3   & F_n'(0)=-2-(n+1)\delta &1
\end{array}
\]
in the last row unless $\delta=-2/(n+1)$.  For
$n\equiv3\pmod4$ one has $U_{n-1}''(0)=n^2-1$, and direct
differentiation of \eqref{eq:flat-F} at that tuned value gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flat-second-derivative}
 F_n''(0)=-2n+\frac{U_{n-1}''(0)}{n+1}=-(n+1)\ne0.
\end{equation}
Thus $\operatorname{ord}_{0}F_n$ is respectively $1$ or $3$ when
$\delta=0$ according as $4\nmid n$ or $4\mid n$, and is respectively
$0$, $1$, $1$, or $2$ in the four defective cases displayed above,
with the final value belonging to the tuned $n\equiv3\pmod4$ case.
Since $P_n=t^{n-1}F_n$, this proves the stated algebraic
multiplicities, including the tuned multiplicity $n+1$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Geometric multiplicity.}
The bulk equations at $t=0$ read
\[
 u_j^-=-u_{j-1}^+\qquad(j\ne0).
\]
They determine every minus component except $u_0^-$ from the plus
components.  Put $\delta=\omega_0-1$.  The two defect equations are
\[
 \delta u_0^+-\omega_0u_0^-=u_{n-1}^+,
 \qquad
 \delta u_0^--\omega_0u_0^+=u_1^-=-u_0^+.
\]
The second equation is therefore
\[
 \delta(u_0^--u_0^+)=0.
\]
If $\omega_0=1$, the first equation supplies
$u_0^-=-u_{n-1}^+$ and leaves the $n$ plus components free.  If
$\omega_0\ne1$, the second equation gives $u_0^-=u_0^+$, and the first
then gives
\[
 u_{n-1}^+=(\delta-\omega_0)u_0^+=-u_0^+.
\]
Thus the geometric multiplicity is $n$ in the homogeneous case and
$n-1$ otherwise.  Fourier decomposition
shows that the two excess algebraic dimensions when $4\mid n$ and
$\delta=0$ are the two $q=\pm\pi/2$ blocks, each a $J_2$.  For
$\delta\ne0$, the parity factorization places the $4\mid n$ excess in
one protected $J_2$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Size of the nontrivial defective block.}
It remains to distinguish one $J_3$ from two $J_2$ blocks in the tuned
$n\equiv3\pmod4$ case.  Put $N_0=L_n(1,1)+2I$ and
$N=L_n(1,\omega_0)+2I=N_0-\delta dd^{\mathsf T}$.  Because
$4\nmid n$, zero is a semisimple eigenvalue of $N_0$ with eigenspace
$E=\ker N_0$ of dimension $n$.  Let $\Pi$ be its spectral projection.
Write
\[
 \det(tI-N_0)=t^nh(t),\qquad h(0)\ne0.
\]
Semisimplicity gives
$(tI-N_0)^{-1}=t^{-1}\Pi+O(1)$.  The rank-one determinant formula then
shows that the coefficient of $t^{n-1}$ in $\det(tI-N)$ is
$\delta h(0)d^{\mathsf T}\Pi d$.
The multiplicity computation above shows that this coefficient vanishes
for $n\equiv3\pmod4$, and therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flat-isotropic-projection}
 d^{\mathsf T}\Pi d=0.
\end{equation}
The bulk relations derived above show directly that the eigenspace of $N$ is
$K=E\cap\ker d^{\mathsf T}$, which has dimension $n-1$.  If
$v\in K\cap\operatorname{ran}N$, write $v=Nu$ and apply $\Pi$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flat-range-line}
 v=\Pi Nu=-\delta\,\Pi d\,(d^{\mathsf T}u).
\end{equation}
Thus $K\cap\operatorname{ran}N$ has dimension at most one.  For a finite
matrix, every Jordan block of size at least two contributes its leading
eigenvector to $\ker N\cap\operatorname{ran}N$, these vectors are
independent, and they span that intersection.  The algebraic multiplicity
exceeds the geometric multiplicity here, so the intersection has
dimension one.  Hence exactly one Jordan block at zero for $N$ is
nontrivial.  It is a $J_2$ when the algebraic multiplicity is $n$, and
the additional algebraic order at $\delta=-2/(n+1)$ makes that same
block a $J_3$.  This proves all six cases and excludes the alternative
of two $J_2$ blocks in the tuned case.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Affected roots away from the flat band.}
Put
$A_-^{(0)}=L^{(0)}|_{\mathcal H_-}$ and consider an affected eigenvalue
$\lambda\ne-2$.
If $\delta\ne0$, it cannot equal an eigenvalue away from $-2$ of the
homogeneous affected restriction $A_-^{(0)}$: the corresponding residue in
\eqref{eq:Gfinite} is nonzero, so
$Q_{n,-}$ does not vanish at that pole.  Hence
$\lambda I-A_-^{(0)}$ is invertible.  The rank-one eigenvalue equation
makes its eigenspace the one-dimensional span of
$(\lambda I-A_-^{(0)})^{-1}d$.  Its root order therefore gives the size
of its unique Jordan block.  If $\delta=0$, all roots away from $-2$ are
simple by Lemma~\ref{lem:no-coincidences}.  This proves the final classification
without asserting cyclicity at the removed flat primary component.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite-ring algebraic exceptional locus]\label{cor:resultant}
For fixed $n$ and $\omega\ne1$, the affected exceptional
parameters are exactly those satisfying
\[
 \Res_\lambda\bigl(Q_{n,-},\partial_\lambda Q_{n,-}\bigr)=0,
\]
with $Q_{n,-}$ given by \eqref{eq:Qminus-poly}.  At $\omega=1$, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flat-affected-order}
r_-=\begin{cases}
 (n+1)/2,&\omega_0=1,\ n\text{ odd},\\
 n/2,&\omega_0=1,\ n\equiv2\pmod4,\\
 n/2+1,&\omega_0=1,\ n\equiv0\pmod4,\\
 (n-1)/2,&\omega_0\ne1,\ n\equiv1\pmod4,\\
 n/2-1,&\omega_0\ne1,\ n\text{ even},\\
 (n+1)/2,&\omega_0\ne1,\ n\equiv3\pmod4,
       \ \omega_0\ne(n-1)/(n+1),\\
 (n+3)/2,&n\equiv3\pmod4,\ \omega_0=(n-1)/(n+1),
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
and define
\[
 \widehat Q_{n,-}(\lambda)
   =\frac{Q_{n,-}(\lambda)}{(\lambda+2)^{r_-}}.
\]
If $n=3$ and $\omega_0=1/2$, then $r_-=3$ and
$\widehat Q_{3,-}=1$, so there are no affected eigenvalues away from
$\lambda=-2$.
On every other parameter stratum in \eqref{eq:flat-affected-order}, the
affected exceptional parameters away from $\lambda=-2$ are exactly those
satisfying
\[
 \Res_\lambda\bigl(\widehat Q_{n,-},
                  \partial_\lambda\widehat Q_{n,-}\bigr)=0.
\]
The protected exceptional parameters \eqref{eq:protected-ep} and the
flat-band cases of Theorem~\ref{thm:flat-band} are listed separately;
they are not asserted to arise from these affected-sector resultants.
Root orders and Theorems~\ref{thm:jordan-regular}--\ref{thm:flat-band}
distinguish exceptional
points from semisimple parity crossings without a numerical tolerance.
For the general affected sector, this is an implicit algebraic
classification: the resultant is an exact finite polynomial equation
for the exceptional locus, and the root-order tests determine the
Jordan block, but no parametric solution of that resultant equation is
asserted.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For a nonconstant polynomial $F$, the resultant
$\Res_\lambda(F,F')$ vanishes exactly when $F$ has a repeated root.
For $\omega\ne1$, the polynomial $Q_{n,-}$ is monic of degree $n$, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:jordan-regular} proves the first assertion.  At
$\omega=1$, it remains to verify the exponent in
\eqref{eq:flat-affected-order}.  Definition~\ref{def:parity-polynomials}
gives
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_{\lambda=-2}H_{n,+}=\begin{cases}
  (n-1)/2,&n\text{ odd},\\
  n/2,&n\equiv2\pmod4,\\
  n/2+1,&n\equiv0\pmod4.
 \end{cases}
\]
Subtracting these protected orders from the total flat algebraic
multiplicities in Theorem~\ref{thm:flat-band} gives every line of
\eqref{eq:flat-affected-order}.  Thus $\widehat Q_{n,-}$ removes the
exact affected flat primary factor on each stratum.  At the tuned
$n=3$ point, $Q_{3,-}$ is monic of degree three and its exact flat order
is three, so $\widehat Q_{3,-}=1$ and the affected spectrum away from
$\lambda=-2$ is empty.  On every other stratum, $\widehat Q_{n,-}$ is
nonconstant, and the resultant criterion applies.
Theorems~\ref{thm:jordan-regular}--\ref{thm:flat-band} then identify the
Jordan block attached to every repeated root away from $-2$ and separately
account for the protected and flat contributions.
\end{proof}

\section{Defect-localized modes}\label{sec:localization}

\subsection{Bulk spectrum and Green function}

\begin{definition}[Bulk spectral set]\label{def:bulk-set}
For the infinite translation-invariant chain, define
\[
 \Sigma_\omega=
 \left\{c-1-\omega\pm\sqrt{\omega^2-(1-c^2)}:
              c\in[-1,1]\right\}.
\]
When $a\ne0$, the identity $D_k=2a(x-c_k)$, with $c_k$ replaced by a
continuous parameter $c\in[-1,1]$, shows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bulk-x-equivalence}
 \lambda\in\Sigma_\omega\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 x(\lambda)\in[-1,1].
\end{equation}
For $\lambda\notin\Sigma_\omega$ with $a\ne0$, retain $a$ and $x$ from
\eqref{eq:a-def}--\eqref{eq:x-def}, and choose
\[
 s(\lambda)=\sqrt{x(\lambda)^2-1}
\]
as the standard branch on $x\in\C\setminus[-1,1]$ satisfying
$s/x\to1$ as $|x|\to\infty$.  Equivalently, it is the branch satisfying
$s(\lambda)\sim x(\lambda)$ as $|\lambda|\to\infty$.  Put $z=x-s$, so
$|z|<1$.

Let $L_\infty^{(0)}$ act on $\ell^2(\Z;\C^2)$ by
\eqref{eq:generator-plus}--\eqref{eq:generator-minus} with every local
rate equal to $\omega$, and let
$L_\infty=L_\infty^{(0)}-\delta dd^{\mathsf T}$, where now $d$ is the
polarization vector at the origin.  These operators are bounded because
they are finite linear combinations of bilateral shifts and bounded
multiplication operators.  Infinite-line eigenvalues below always mean
$\ell^2$ eigenvalues of $L_\infty$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Bulk spectrum and cyclic Green identities]
\label{lem:green-identities}
The homogeneous infinite-chain spectrum is exactly
\[
 \spec L_\infty^{(0)}=\Sigma_\omega.
\]
For $x\in\C\setminus[-1,1]$, with $s=\sqrt{x^2-1}$ and $z=x-s$ on
the branch of Definition~\ref{def:bulk-set},
\begin{align}
 \frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{dq}{x-\cos q}
   &=\frac1s,\label{eq:green-circle-integral}\\
 \frac1n\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac1{x-\cos q_k}
   &=\frac1s\frac{1+z^n}{1-z^n}.
   \label{eq:green-root-unity}
\end{align}
The right side of \eqref{eq:green-root-unity} is invariant under the
simultaneous branch change $(s,z)\mapsto(-s,z^{-1})$ and therefore
extends meromorphically to every $x\in\C$ away from the finite set
$\{\cos q_k:0\le k<n\}$, where it equals the finite sum on the left.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The unitary Fourier transform identifies $L_\infty^{(0)}$ with
multiplication on $L^2([0,2\pi];\C^2)$ by the continuous matrix symbol
\[
 W(q)=\begin{pmatrix}
 e^{\ii q}-1-\omega&\omega\\
 \omega&e^{-\ii q}-1-\omega
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its two pointwise eigenvalues are
\[
 \cos q-1-\omega\pm\sqrt{\omega^2-\sin^2q},
\]
whose union is $\Sigma_\omega$.  If $\lambda\notin\Sigma_\omega$,
then $\lambda I-W(q)$ is invertible for every $q$ and its inverse is a
continuous matrix function on the compact circle, hence is uniformly
bounded and defines $(\lambda-L_\infty^{(0)})^{-1}$.  Conversely, if
$\lambda$ is a pointwise eigenvalue at $q_0$, choose a corresponding
nonzero vector and normalized scalar functions supported in shrinking
neighborhoods of $q_0$.  The resulting Fourier-space vectors form an
approximate eigenvector sequence by continuity of $W$, so
$\lambda\in\spec L_\infty^{(0)}$.  This proves the spectral identity.

For \eqref{eq:green-circle-integral}, put $\zeta=e^{\ii q}$.  Since
$z+z^{-1}=2x$ and $|z|<1$, the two roots of the resulting denominator
are $z$ and $z^{-1}$, and only $z$ lies inside the unit circle.  The
residue theorem gives
\[
 \frac1{2\pi}\oint_{|\zeta|=1}
   \frac{2\ii\,d\zeta}{(\zeta-z)(\zeta-z^{-1})}
 =\frac{2}{z^{-1}-z}=\frac1s.
\]
Also, the absolutely convergent Poisson-kernel expansion is
\[
 \frac1{x-\cos q}
 =\frac1s\left(1+2\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}z^r\cos(rq)\right).
\]
Averaging over $q_k=2\pi k/n$ removes every term for which $n\nmid r$.
Summing the remaining geometric series proves
\eqref{eq:green-root-unity}.  Finally, the displayed branch change
fixes its right side.  It consequently defines a single-valued
meromorphic function, which agrees with the finite rational sum on
$\C\setminus[-1,1]$ and hence everywhere away from their poles.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Embedded point spectrum and singular flat band]
\label{prop:embedded-spectrum}
Let $\lambda\in\Sigma_\omega$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $a=\lambda+1+\omega\ne0$, then
  $\ker_{\ell^2}(L_\infty-\lambda I)=\{0\}$.
\item If $a=0$, then necessarily $\omega=1$ and $\lambda=-2$.  For every
  $\omega_0\ge0$, the embedded flat-band eigenspace is exactly
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:infinite-flat-eigenspace}
  \ker_{\ell^2}(L_\infty+2I)
  =\left\{u:\
    \begin{array}{l}
    u_j^+=p_j,\quad u_j^-=-p_{j-1},\quad p\in\ell^2(\Z),\\
    (\omega_0-1)(p_0+p_{-1})=0
    \end{array}\right\}.
  \end{equation}
  This eigenspace is infinite dimensional.  Its compactly supported
  vectors are precisely those obtained from finitely supported $p$.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the singular flat band is the only embedded $\ell^2$ point spectrum;
all other infinite-line localized eigenvalues lie outside
$\Sigma_\omega$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $a\ne0$.  By
\eqref{eq:bulk-x-equivalence}, $x\in[-1,1]$.  For an eigenvector $u$, put
\[
 y_j=\binom{u_j^+}{u_{j+1}^-}.
\]
Away from the defect, the transfer relation in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:infinite-bulk-transfer}
 y_j=T_\omega(\lambda)y_{j-1},\qquad j\ne0.
\end{equation}
The matrix $T_\omega$ is unimodular and has trace $2x$.  If
$x\in(-1,1)$, its two eigenvalues are distinct and lie on the unit
circle, so the families $T_\omega^m$ and $T_\omega^{-m}$ are uniformly
bounded.  Consequently, neither a forward nor a backward orbit can tend
to zero unless its initial vector is zero.  At
$x=\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}$, Cayley--Hamilton gives
\[
 T_\omega=\varepsilon I+N,\qquad N^2=0,
 \qquad
 T_\omega^m=\varepsilon^m
   \bigl(I+m\varepsilon^{-1}N\bigr).
\]
If $Nv=0$, then $\|T_\omega^mv\|=\|v\|$; if $Nv\ne0$, then
\[
 \|T_\omega^mv\|\ge m\|Nv\|-\|v\|\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
The identity
$T_\omega^{-m}=\varepsilon^{-m}(I-m\varepsilon^{-1}N)$ gives the same
alternatives for negative powers.  Hence an orbit in either direction
tends to zero only when $v=0$.

Because $u\in\ell^2$, one has $y_j\to0$ as $j\to\pm\infty$.
Applying the preceding orbit alternatives to the right half-line in
\eqref{eq:infinite-bulk-transfer} gives $y_0=0$, and applying them to the
left half-line gives $y_{-1}=0$.  These two identities and the recurrence
force every component of $u$ to vanish.  This proves the first item.

Now suppose $a=0$.  Then $\lambda=-1-\omega$ and the bulk determinant is
$D(q)=1-\omega^2$, independent of $q$.  Membership in
$\Sigma_\omega$ therefore forces $\omega=1$ and $\lambda=-2$.
Set $p_j=u_j^+$.  At every bulk site the eigenvalue equations reduce to
\[
 u_j^-=-p_{j-1},\qquad u_{j+1}^-=-p_j.
\]
The equations adjacent to the defect supply the same relation across both
interfaces, so $u_j^-=-p_{j-1}$ for every $j\in\Z$.  After this
substitution, the two equations at the defect are both equivalent to
\[
 (\omega_0-1)(p_0+p_{-1})=0.
\]
Conversely, any $p\in\ell^2(\Z)$ satisfying this scalar condition gives
an $\ell^2$ eigenvector by direct substitution.  This proves
\eqref{eq:infinite-flat-eigenspace}.  The condition cuts out either all of
$\ell^2(\Z)$ or one codimension-one subspace, so the eigenspace is infinite
dimensional; the support assertion follows from the displayed shift
relation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Infinite-line defect modes}

A localized mode whose support is not finite is called a
\emph{noncompact localized mode}.  In the decay-root parametrization below
this is the range $0<|z|<1$; the compact case $z=0$ is treated separately.

\begin{theorem}[Infinite-line off-bulk localization criterion]
\label{thm:localization}
Let $\delta\ne0$, $\lambda\notin\Sigma_\omega$, and $a\ne0$.  Then
$\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $L_\infty$ admitting an exponentially
localized $\ell^2$ eigenvector if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localization-secular}
 1+2\delta g_\infty(\lambda)=0,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localization-green}
 g_\infty(\lambda)
 =\frac1{2a}\left(1+\frac{\lambda+1-x}{s}\right)
 =\frac{z(\lambda+1-z)}{a(1-z^2)}.
\end{equation}
The localization length is
\[
 \xi(\lambda)=\frac{-1}{\log|z|}.
\]
Equivalently, with $a=\lambda+1+\omega$, the pair $(a,z)$ satisfies
\begin{align}
 a^2+1-\omega^2-a(z+z^{-1})&=0,\label{eq:loc-dispersion}\\
 a(1-z^2)+2\delta z(a-\omega-z)&=0,
 \qquad 0<|z|<1.\label{eq:loc-match}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The polarization entry of the bulk resolvent at the defect site is
\begin{align*}
 g_\infty(\lambda)
 &=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
   \frac{\lambda+1-\cos q}
        {a^2+1-\omega^2-2a\cos q}\,dq\\
 &=\frac1{2a}\left[1+(\lambda+1-x)
       \frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{dq}{x-\cos q}\right]\\
 &=\frac1{2a}\left(1+\frac{\lambda+1-x}{s}\right).
\end{align*}
Here the last equality is \eqref{eq:green-circle-integral}.
If $(\lambda-L_\infty)u=0$, then
\[
 u=-\delta(\lambda-L_\infty^{(0)})^{-1}d\,(d^{\mathsf T}u).
\]
The scalar $d^{\mathsf T}u$ cannot vanish unless $u=0$.  Applying
$d^{\mathsf T}$ therefore gives \eqref{eq:localization-secular}.
Conversely, when that scalar equation holds, the displayed resolvent
vector is a nonzero $\ell^2$ eigenvector.  Since
$x=(z+z^{-1})/2$ and $s=(z^{-1}-z)/2$, elementary simplification gives
the second expression in \eqref{eq:localization-green}.  The Fourier
coefficient of $(x-\cos q)^{-1}$ is $z^{|j|}/s$; consequently, on the
half-line $j\ge0$, the second component of
$(\lambda-L_\infty^{(0)})^{-1}d$ is
\[
 -\frac{\lambda+1-z}{2as}\,z^j.
\]
The secular equation and $\delta\ne0$ give
$g_\infty(\lambda)=-1/(2\delta)\ne0$.  The second identity in
\eqref{eq:localization-green}, together with $a\ne0$, $z\ne0$, and
$|z|<1$, therefore gives $\lambda+1-z\ne0$.  This tail does not cancel,
so its exact exponential rate is $|z|$ and the localization length is
$-1/\log|z|$.  Clearing the denominator in
\eqref{eq:localization-secular} gives
\eqref{eq:loc-match}; the bulk dispersion relation gives
\eqref{eq:loc-dispersion}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Algebraic candidates for noncompact localized modes]
\label{cor:localization-algebraic}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:localization}, every
admissible $z$ is a root of
\begin{align}
0={}&2\delta z^3+(2\delta\omega+1-\omega^2)z^2\notag\\
&+(4\delta^2\omega+4\delta\omega^2-2\delta)z
 +(2\delta\omega+\omega^2-1),\label{eq:localization-cubic}
\end{align}
and, when the denominator is nonzero,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localization-recovery}
 a=\frac{2\delta z(\omega+z)}{1-z^2+2\delta z},
 \qquad \lambda=a-1-\omega.
\end{equation}
The zero-denominator branch in \eqref{eq:localization-recovery} occurs
precisely when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localization-zero-denominator}
 0<\omega<1,\qquad
 \omega_0=\frac{\omega^2+1}{2\omega},\qquad z=-\omega.
\end{equation}
At these parameters there are two admissible values
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localization-zero-denominator-roots}
 a_\pm=\frac{-\omega^2-1
       \pm\sqrt{5\omega^4-2\omega^2+1}}{2\omega},
 \qquad \lambda_\pm=a_\pm-1-\omega,
\end{equation}
and both give noncompact localized modes with decay root
$z=-\omega$.
A root of \eqref{eq:localization-cubic} is accepted only when both
\eqref{eq:loc-dispersion} and \eqref{eq:loc-match} hold; this removes
roots introduced by elimination or by clearing denominators.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
After multiplication of the dispersion relation by $z$, the elimination
factorization is
\[
 \operatorname{Res}_a\!\left(
  za^2+z(1-\omega^2)-a(z^2+1),
  a(1-z^2+2\delta z)-2\delta z(\omega+z)
 \right)
 =z(z^2-1)\mathcal C_{\omega,\delta}(z),
\]
where $\mathcal C_{\omega,\delta}$ is the cubic polynomial displayed in
\eqref{eq:localization-cubic}.  The admissible noncompact range
$0<|z|<1$ excludes the three prefactors: $z=\pm1$ are bulk-edge
points, while $z=0$ is evaluated separately below.  Thus eliminating
$a$ gives \eqref{eq:localization-cubic} on the stated range.  Solving
\eqref{eq:loc-match} for $a$ gives \eqref{eq:localization-recovery}.

Put $C=1-z^2+2\delta z$.  If $C=0$, the matching equation reduces to
$2\delta z(\omega+z)=0$.  Since $\delta\ne0$ and $z\ne0$, this forces
$z=-\omega$, and then $C=0$ is equivalent to
$\delta=(1-\omega^2)/(2\omega)$.  The condition $|z|<1$ is exactly
$0<\omega<1$.  Substitution in the dispersion equation gives
\[
 a^2+(\omega+\omega^{-1})a+1-\omega^2=0,
\]
whose two roots are the values in
\eqref{eq:localization-zero-denominator-roots}.  Conversely, each root
satisfies both \eqref{eq:loc-dispersion} and \eqref{eq:loc-match}.
At this tuning the cubic itself factors as
\[
 \frac{1-\omega^2}{\omega}\,z(z+\omega)^2,
\]
so its double nonzero root accounts for the two values of $a$ rather
than for a repeated eigenvalue.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Compact singular-transfer mode]
\label{prop:compact-localization}
Let $\omega\ne1$.  The value $z=0$, excluded from the noncompact
parametrization, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compact-localization}
 \lambda=-1-\omega
 \quad\text{as a localized eigenvalue if and only if}\quad
 \omega_0=\frac{\omega^2+1}{2\omega}.
\end{equation}
Its localization length is $\xi=0$ by the finite-support convention, and
its eigenvector is supported at the defect and its two neighboring sites.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At $\lambda=-1-\omega$ the Fourier denominator is the nonzero constant
$1-\omega^2$, and hence
\[
 g_\infty(-1-\omega)
 =\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
     \frac{-\omega-\cos q}{1-\omega^2}\,dq
 =\frac{\omega}{\omega^2-1}.
\]
The secular equation is therefore equivalent to
$\delta=(1-\omega^2)/(2\omega)$, which is exactly
\eqref{eq:compact-localization}.  In Fourier variables the associated
resolvent vector is proportional to
\[
 \binom{-\omega-e^{-\ii q}}{\omega+e^{\ii q}}.
\]
Its inverse Fourier transform is supported at the defect and its two
neighbors.  This proves the compact case and also shows directly that
it is the limiting root $z=0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Return to finite rings}

\begin{proposition}[Finite-ring convergence of a localized mode]
\label{prop:localized-convergence}
Suppose \eqref{eq:localization-secular} has a simple root
$\lambda_\infty$ with decay root
$z_\infty$, $0<|z_\infty|<1$.  There is a closed disk $B$ centered at
$\lambda_\infty$, disjoint from $\Sigma_\omega$, such that, for all
sufficiently large $n$, the finite polynomial $Q_{n,-}$ has exactly one
zero $\lambda_n$ in the interior of $B$, counted with algebraic
multiplicity, and
\[
 \lambda_n-\lambda_\infty=O(|z_\infty|^n).
\]
If the corresponding right eigenvector is normalized in $\ell^2$, define
its squared site amplitude by
$M_j(u)=|u_j^+|^2+|u_j^-|^2$.  This is a right-eigenvector localization
diagnostic, not a probability law or stationary mass of the Markov chain;
no assertion about left-eigenvector localization is made.  The amplitude
is bounded by $C|z_\infty|^{2\operatorname{dist}(j,0)}$ for a constant $C$
independent of $j$ and of all sufficiently large $n$.
In the compact case \eqref{eq:compact-localization}, the infinite and
finite eigenvalues agree exactly and the eigenvector is supported where
$\operatorname{dist}(j,0)\le1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Eigenvalue convergence.}
Equation \eqref{eq:green-root-unity} applied to the bulk Green kernel
gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-green-z}
 G_n(\lambda)=\frac1{2a}\left[1+
  \frac{\lambda+1-x}{s}\frac{1+z^n}{1-z^n}\right].
\end{equation}
Under the simultaneous branch change $(s,z)\mapsto(-s,z^{-1})$, the
last two factors in the second term both change sign.  The right-hand
side is therefore branch invariant and continues meromorphically as
the finite rational function $G_n$ to every point away from a
finite-ring homogeneous pole.  This continuation statement concerns
$G_n$ only; the infinite-volume resolvent entry $g_\infty$ remains
defined on $\mathbb C\setminus\Sigma_\omega$.  On the closed disk chosen
below, which is disjoint from $\Sigma_\omega$, use the common branch of
$s$ and $z$.  Subtracting \eqref{eq:localization-green} from
\eqref{eq:finite-green-z} then gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-green-error}
 G_n-g_\infty=
 \frac{\lambda+1-x}{as}\frac{z^n}{1-z^n}.
\end{equation}
Choose a closed disk $B$ about $\lambda_\infty$ disjoint from
$\Sigma_\omega$ and containing no other zero of
$1+2\delta g_\infty$.  Shrinking it if necessary, choose
\[
 \rho_B=\max_{\lambda\in B}|z(\lambda)|<r<1.
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:homogeneous-polynomial}, every zero of
$H_{n,-}^{(0)}$ is a finite-ring homogeneous Fourier eigenvalue and
therefore belongs to $\Sigma_\omega$.  Hence $H_{n,-}^{(0)}$ is
nonzero on $B$, and the zeros of $1+2\delta G_n$ in $B$ are exactly
the zeros of $Q_{n,-}$ there.
Equation
\eqref{eq:finite-green-error} and its $\lambda$-derivative are bounded
on $B$ by $C_Br^n$: for the derivative, the only additional factor is
$n\rho_B^{n-1}=O(r^n)$.  Rouch\'e's theorem on $\partial B$, followed by
the simple-zero estimate at $\lambda_\infty$, gives one zero
$\lambda_n$ and first yields
$\lambda_n-\lambda_\infty=O(r^n)$.  The root equation and
\eqref{eq:finite-green-error} then give
\[
 |\lambda_n-\lambda_\infty|
 \le C|z(\lambda_n)|^n.
\]
Since $z_\infty\ne0$ and
$z(\lambda_n)/z_\infty=1+O(r^n)$, one has
$|z(\lambda_n)/z_\infty|^n=\exp(O(nr^n))=O(1)$.  Hence
$\lambda_n-\lambda_\infty=O(|z_\infty|^n)$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Uniform localization.}
Put $\rho_\infty=|z_\infty|$, $z_n=z(\lambda_n)$, and
$s_n=s(\lambda_n)$.  Averaging the Poisson-kernel expansion in
Lemma~\ref{lem:green-identities} against $e^{\ii jq_k}$ gives, for
the representative $j\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localized-aliased-coefficient}
 \frac1n\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}
   \frac{e^{\ii jq_k}}{x(\lambda_n)-\cos q_k}
 =\frac1{s_n}\sum_{\ell\in\Z}z_n^{|j+\ell n|}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the Fourier coefficient on the infinite circle is
$z_n^{|j|}/s_n$, and root-of-unity averaging retains exactly the
indices congruent to $-j$ modulo $n$.  Moreover, in Fourier variables,
\[
 (\lambda_n-W(q))^{-1}d
 =\frac1{2a_n(x(\lambda_n)-\cos q)}
   \binom{\lambda_n+1-e^{-\ii q}}
         {-\lambda_n-1+e^{\ii q}},
 \qquad a_n=\lambda_n+1+\omega.
\]
Thus each component of the finite-ring resolvent vector is a bounded
linear combination of the coefficients in
\eqref{eq:localized-aliased-coefficient} at $j$ and $j\pm1$.  If
$h_j=\operatorname{dist}(j,0)\le n/2$, then the geometric series gives
the exact majorant
\[
 \sum_{\ell\in\Z}|z_n|^{|j+\ell n|}
 =\frac{|z_n|^{h_j}+|z_n|^{n-h_j}}{1-|z_n|^n}.
\]
Since $s_n$ and $a_n$ stay away from zero and
$|z_n|\ge\rho_\infty/2$ for
large $n$, the shifts by one only alter the constant.  Consequently
the periodized resolvent components are bounded by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localized-periodization-bound}
 C\frac{|z_n|^{h_j}+|z_n|^{n-h_j}}{1-|z_n|^n},
 \qquad h_j=\operatorname{dist}(j,0)\le n/2,
\end{equation}
where $C$ is independent of $j$ and of all sufficiently large $n$.
Analyticity of $z(\lambda)$ near the simple off-spectrum eigenvalue and
the eigenvalue estimate already proved give
\[
 z_n=z_\infty+O(\rho_\infty^n),\qquad
 \frac{|z_n|}{\rho_\infty}\le1+C\rho_\infty^{n-1}.
\]
It follows uniformly for $h_j\le n/2$ that
\[
 \left(\frac{|z_n|}{\rho_\infty}\right)^{h_j}
 \le \exp(Ch_j\rho_\infty^{n-1})
 \le \exp(Cn\rho_\infty^{n-1}/2)=O(1).
\]
For large $n$, $|z_n|<1$ and $n-h_j\ge h_j$, so the wrapped term in
\eqref{eq:localized-periodization-bound} is no larger than its first
term; also $1-|z_n|^n$ is bounded below.  Thus every component is
bounded by $C\rho_\infty^{h_j}$.  The local coefficients converge
componentwise to the nonzero infinite-volume resolvent vector.  Choose a
fixed site and component whose limiting coefficient is nonzero.  That
coefficient stays bounded away from zero, while the geometric majorant above
bounds the entire unnormalized $\ell^2$ norm from above.  Hence this norm is
bounded above and below by positive constants.  Normalization therefore
changes the component bound only by a fixed factor, and squaring the two
components proves the squared site-amplitude bound
$C\rho_\infty^{2h_j}$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Compact case.}
In the compact case, the Laurent-polynomial Fourier vector displayed in
the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:compact-localization} is an exact finite-ring
eigenvector for every $n$ (with the two neighbors identified when
$n=2$), proving the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\section{Fixed-rate spectral gap}\label{sec:fixed-gap}

At fixed $\omega>0$, the first spatial mode has decay of order $n^{-2}$
and reflection protects one of its parity components.  The local pole
calculation identifies the protected and affected candidate roots; the
right-edge estimates below show that no other root has a larger real
part.  Section~\ref{sec:critical-gap} treats the distinct bulk-critical
regime in which both tumbling rates scale as $n^{-1}$.

\subsection{Gap definition}

\begin{lemma}[Irreducibility]\label{lem:irreducibility}
For every $n\ge2$, $\omega>0$, and $\omega_0\ge0$, the directed transition
graph of $L_n(\omega,\omega_0)$ is strongly connected.  Consequently zero
is an algebraically simple eigenvalue of $L_n(\omega,\omega_0)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose a bulk site $r\ne0$.  Starting from any state, repeated hops in its
current orientation reach $r$.  At $r$ the orientation can be retained or
reversed, the latter transition having rate $\omega>0$.  Repeated hops in
the selected orientation then reach any prescribed target site with the
prescribed orientation.  Thus every state communicates with every other
state, including when $\omega_0=0$.

For every $t>0$, each such finite path has positive probability of being
completed before time $t$, so every entry of $e^{tL_n}$ is positive.  This
matrix is stochastic.  The Perron--Frobenius theorem therefore makes its
eigenvalue one algebraically simple.  A nontrivial Jordan chain of $L_n$ at
zero would exponentiate to a nontrivial Jordan chain of $e^{tL_n}$ at one,
which is impossible.  Hence zero is algebraically simple.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Spectral gap]\label{def:gap}
For $\omega>0$ and $n\ge2$, Lemma~\ref{lem:irreducibility} makes zero a
simple eigenvalue.  Define
\[
 \gamma_n(\omega,\omega_0)
 =-\max\{\Re\lambda:\lambda\in\spec L_n(\omega,\omega_0),
                            \ \lambda\ne0\}.
\]
Put $q=2\pi/n$ and define the protected first-mode decay rate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hom-gap}
 \gamma_n^{\rm hom}(\omega)
 =-\Re\lambda_1^+
 =1-\cos q+\omega-\Re\sqrt{\omega^2-\sin^2q},
\end{equation}
where the principal square root is used.  For each fixed $\omega>0$,
the radicand is nonnegative for all sufficiently large $n$, and this
rate then equals the homogeneous spectral gap.  No such identity is
asserted for small $n$, where the first-mode roots can be nonreal and
the tumble mode can be slower.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Spectral gap and semigroup decay]
\label{prop:gap-semigroup}
Let $\pi_n$ be the stationary probability vector, let $\one$ be the
all-ones column vector, and put
$\Pi_n=\pi_n\one^{\mathsf T}$.  Then $\gamma_n>0$, and $\Pi_n$ is the
spectral projection of $L_n$ at zero.  Let $r_n$ be the largest size of
a Jordan block among the eigenvalues satisfying
$\Re\lambda=-\gamma_n$.  For every submultiplicative matrix norm there
is a constant $C_n$ such that, for $t\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:semigroup-gap-bound}
 e^{-\gamma_nt}
 \le \lVert e^{tL_n}-\Pi_n\rVert
 \le C_n(1+t^{r_n-1})e^{-\gamma_nt}.
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:semigroup-gap-exponent}
 \lim_{t\to\infty}\frac1t
  \log\lVert e^{tL_n}-\Pi_n\rVert=-\gamma_n.
\end{equation}
If $r_n>1$ and
$(v_0,\ldots,v_{r_n-1})$ is a corresponding Jordan chain, ordered by
$(L_n-\lambda I)v_0=0$ and
$(L_n-\lambda I)v_j=v_{j-1}$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:semigroup-jordan-chain}
 e^{tL_n}v_{r_n-1}
 =e^{\lambda t}\sum_{k=0}^{r_n-1}\frac{t^k}{k!}v_{r_n-1-k}.
\end{equation}
Thus $\gamma_n$ is the asymptotic exponential decay rate; the Jordan
classification determines possible polynomial factors, while transient
constants and total-variation mixing times are not specified by
$\gamma_n$ alone.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:irreducibility} shows that $e^{sL_n}$ is a positive
stochastic matrix for every $s>0$.  The strict Perron--Frobenius theorem
places every eigenvalue of $e^{sL_n}$ other than its simple eigenvalue one
strictly inside the unit circle.  By spectral mapping,
$e^{s\lambda}$ is an eigenvalue of $e^{sL_n}$ for every
$\lambda\in\spec L_n$; hence every nonzero eigenvalue of $L_n$ has
negative real part and $\gamma_n>0$.

The normalization $\one^{\mathsf T}\pi_n=1$, together with
$L_n\pi_n=0$ and $\one^{\mathsf T}L_n=0$, identifies the rank-one
spectral projection at zero as $\Pi_n$.  Put $L_n$ into Jordan normal
form on the complementary generalized eigenspaces.  A block of size
$r$ at $\lambda$ contributes
$e^{\lambda t}\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}t^kN^k/k!$.  The blocks on the spectral
line $\Re\lambda=-\gamma_n$ therefore give the upper bound in
\eqref{eq:semigroup-gap-bound}; polynomial factors from blocks strictly
to the left are absorbed into its constant.  The spectrum of
$e^{tL_n}-\Pi_n$ consists of zero and the numbers $e^{t\lambda}$ for
$\lambda\ne0$, so its spectral radius is $e^{-\gamma_nt}$ and is bounded
above by every submultiplicative matrix norm.  This gives the lower
bound.  Taking logarithms proves
\eqref{eq:semigroup-gap-exponent}, and the Jordan-block exponential is
exactly \eqref{eq:semigroup-jordan-chain}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Fixed-rate asymptotics}

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-rate gap asymptotics]\label{thm:fixed-gap}
Fix $\omega>0$ and $\omega_0\ge0$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $\omega_0\le\omega$, then for every sufficiently large $n$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:slow-defect-gap}
    \gamma_n(\omega,\omega_0)=\gamma_n^{\rm hom}(\omega).
  \end{equation}
\item If $\omega_0>\omega$, then
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:fast-defect-gap}
 \gamma_n(\omega,\omega_0)
  =\gamma_n^{\rm hom}(\omega)
   -\frac{4\pi^2(\omega_0-\omega)(1+\omega)}
          {\omega^2(1+\omega_0)}\,n^{-3}
    +O(|\omega_0-\omega|n^{-4}).
  \end{equation}
\item In both cases,
  \begin{align}
  \gamma_n^{\rm hom}(\omega)
   ={}&\frac{2\pi^2(1+\omega)}{\omega}\,n^{-2}\notag\\
    &-16\pi^4\left(\frac1{24}+\frac1{6\omega}
                    -\frac1{8\omega^3}\right)n^{-4}
      +O(n^{-6}).\label{eq:hom-gap-expansion}
  \end{align}
\end{enumerate}
The errors are uniform when $(\omega,\omega_0)$ ranges over a compact
set with $\omega$ bounded away from zero.  The root selection is uniform
through $\omega_0=\omega$, where the two parity roots coincide.
\end{theorem}

The selection mechanism is visible directly in the two parity sectors.
Reflection fixes the protected first-mode root at its homogeneous value.
The affected companion moves away from that value at order $n^{-3}$:
a slower defect moves it to the left and leaves the protected root in
control, whereas a faster defect moves it toward zero and selects the
affected root.  Thus a local rate change alters the first correction but
not the leading fixed-rate decay scale.

The proof occupies the next two subsections.  The local Green estimate
locates the affected companion of the protected first-mode root; the
right-edge separation then excludes every other extended, band-edge, or
localized eigenvalue.

\subsection{Fixed-rate Green bounds and root separation}

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-rate regular Green estimate]
\label{lem:fixed-regular-green}
Let $K\subset(0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)$ be compact and let $A>0$.
For $(\omega,\omega_0)\in K$, put
\[
 p_n=\lambda_1^+,
 \qquad
 R_n=-\frac{\omega-r_1}{2r_1},
 \qquad r_1=\sqrt{\omega^2-\sin^2(2\pi/n)}.
\]
For all sufficiently large $n$, define the regular part on
$|\lambda-p_n|\le A n^{-3}$ by
\[
 G_n^{\rm reg}(\lambda)
 =G_n(\lambda)-\frac{2R_n}{n(\lambda-p_n)}.
\]
There is $C=C(K,A)$ such that throughout this disk
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-regular-green-bounds}
 \left|G_n^{\rm reg}(\lambda)-\frac1{2(1+\omega)}\right|
 \le \frac Cn,
 \qquad
 \left|(G_n^{\rm reg})'(\lambda)\right|\le Cn.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $q_k^*=2\pi\min(k,n-k)/n$.  Choose $\eta>0$, uniformly for
$(\omega,\omega_0)\in K$, so that
$|\sin q|\le\omega/2$ for $0\le q\le\eta$.  On this interval put
\[
 p^\pm(q)=\cos q-1-\omega
       \pm\sqrt{\omega^2-\sin^2q},
 \qquad
 R^+(q)=-\frac{\omega-\sqrt{\omega^2-\sin^2q}}
                   {2\sqrt{\omega^2-\sin^2q}}.
\]
Taylor's theorem with remainder, applied uniformly on the compact
$\omega$-projection of $K$, gives constants $c,C>0$ such that
\begin{align}
 c q^2&\le -p^+(q)\le Cq^2,
 &|R^+(q)|&\le Cq^2,\label{eq:fixed-small-wave-bounds}\\
 |p^+(q_k^*)-p^+(q_1^*)|
   &\ge c(k^2-1)n^{-2}\qquad(2\le k\le \eta n/(2\pi)).
 \label{eq:fixed-pole-spacing}
\end{align}
For the last inequality, regard $p^+(q)$ as a function of $q^2$;
its derivative at zero is $-(1+\omega)/(2\omega)$ and, after decreasing
$\eta$, its modulus is bounded below by a positive constant.  The fast
root $p^-(q)$ stays a fixed distance from zero on $[0,\eta]$.
For $\eta\le q\le\pi$, both roots stay a fixed distance from zero:
the real-part dissipation of the homogeneous Fourier block can vanish
only at $q=0$ in the density direction, and the Heine--Borel theorem
applied to the remaining compact parameter set supplies a uniform
positive distance.

For an interior paired wave number with distinct roots, partial fractions
give
\[
 \frac{\lambda+1-\cos q_k}{D_k(\lambda)}
 =\frac{R^+(q_k^*)}{\lambda-p^+(q_k^*)}
  +\frac{1-R^+(q_k^*)}{\lambda-p^-(q_k^*)}.
\]
Let $S_n$ be the convex hull of zero and
$\{|\lambda-p_n|\le A n^{-3}\}$.  Every point of $S_n$ lies to the
right of the second slow pole by at least
$c(k^2-1)n^{-2}$ at index $k\ge2$, after decreasing $c$ and increasing
$n$.  Hence equations
\eqref{eq:fixed-small-wave-bounds}--\eqref{eq:fixed-pole-spacing}
bound the derivative of the regular slow-pole sum throughout $S_n$ by
\[
 Cn\sum_{k=2}^\infty\frac{k^2}{(k^2-1)^2}\le C'n.
\]
For $q_k^*\le\eta$, every fast-pole denominator stays uniformly away
from $S_n$.  For $q_k^*\ge\eta$, including a coalesced pair with
$\omega=|\sin q_k|$, use the unreduced summand in
\eqref{eq:Gfinite}.  The compactness argument above gives
$|D_k(\lambda)|\ge c>0$ on $S_n$, uniformly in this range.  The numerator,
$D_k$, and $D_k'$ are uniformly bounded there, so direct differentiation
of $(\lambda+1-\cos q_k)/D_k(\lambda)$ is $O(1)$.  After the $1/n$
normalization, each corresponding paired contribution to $G_n$ is
therefore $O(n^{-1})$, without dividing by the difference of the two
roots.  There are at most $n/2$ such pairs.  The two endpoint
contributions have derivatives $O(n^{-1})$ because their poles stay a
fixed distance from $S_n$.  Thus these terms have total derivative
$O(1)$.  This proves the derivative bound in
\eqref{eq:fixed-regular-green-bounds}.

At $\lambda=0$, every unpaired nonzero-wave Fourier summand is exactly
$1/[2(1+\omega)]$.  The $q=0$ term and the removed first-pole
term each change the average by $O(n^{-1})$, using
\eqref{eq:fixed-small-wave-bounds}.  Hence
$G_n^{\rm reg}(0)=1/[2(1+\omega)]+O(n^{-1})$.
Finally $|p_n|=O(n^{-2})$, so integration of the derivative bound along
the segment from zero to the displayed disk proves the first bound in
\eqref{eq:fixed-regular-green-bounds}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Protected first mode and affected pole]
\label{lem:gap-pole}
For every $n$, the protected sector contains $\lambda_1^+$ and
$\Re\lambda_1^+=-\gamma_n^{\rm hom}$.  Whenever
$|\sin(2\pi/n)|\le\omega$, this root is real and
$\lambda_1^+=-\gamma_n^{\rm hom}$.  Let
$K\subset(0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)$ be compact.  Uniformly for
$(\omega,\omega_0)\in K$ as $n\to\infty$, the affected root associated
with the same homogeneous pole is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affected-gap-shift}
 \lambda_{1,-}=\lambda_1^+
 +\frac{\delta(1+\omega)}{\omega^2(1+\omega_0)}
       \frac{q^2}{n}+O(|\delta|n^{-4}).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The sector assertion is Theorem~\ref{thm:parity-factor}; the two
equalities follow from Lemma~\ref{lem:homogeneous-polynomial} and
Definition~\ref{def:gap}.  Near
$\lambda_1^+$, the two terms $k=1,n-1$ in the full Fourier Green
function have residue
\[
 R_1=\frac{\lambda_1^++1-c_1}{\lambda_1^+-\lambda_1^-}
    =-\frac{\omega-r_1}{2r_1},
 \qquad r_1=\sqrt{\omega^2-\sin^2q}.
\]
Thus
\[
 G_n(\lambda)=\frac{2R_1}{n(\lambda-\lambda_1^+)}
                  +G_n^{\rm reg}(\lambda).
\]
If $\delta=0$, the affected root equals the protected root and the
formula follows.  Suppose $\delta\ne0$ and put
\[
 M(\lambda)=1+2\delta G_n^{\rm reg}(\lambda),
 \qquad M_0=\frac{1+\omega_0}{1+\omega}.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-regular-green} gives, uniformly on every fixed
$O(n^{-3})$ disk about $\lambda_1^+$,
\[
 M(\lambda)=M_0+O(|\delta|n^{-1}),
 \qquad M'(\lambda)=O(|\delta|n).
\]
The pole-cleared secular equation is
\[
 h_n(\lambda):=(\lambda-\lambda_1^+)M(\lambda)
                  +\frac{4\delta R_1}{n}=0.
\]
Its affine comparison has the zero
\[
 \lambda_* -\lambda_1^+=-\frac{4\delta R_1}{nM_0}=O(|\delta|n^{-3}).
\]
On a circle of radius $C|\delta|n^{-4}$ about $\lambda_*$, the
difference between $h_n$ and
$M_0(\lambda-\lambda_*)$ is at most
$C_1\delta^2n^{-4}+O(\delta^2n^{-5})$.  Since $\delta$ ranges over a
fixed bounded set, choosing $C$ larger than $C_1|\delta|/M_0$ makes the
affine term strictly dominant.  Rouch\'e's theorem gives a unique root
in this circle.  Therefore
\[
 \lambda_{1,-}-\lambda_1^+
 =\frac{2\delta(\omega-r_1)}{nr_1}
    \frac{1}{1+\delta/(1+\omega)}+O(|\delta|n^{-4}),
\]
where the constant is uniform on the compact parameter sets of the
lemma.  Finally
$(\omega-r_1)/r_1=q^2/(2\omega^2)+O(q^4)$, which proves
\eqref{eq:affected-gap-shift}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-rate threshold Green bounds]
\label{lem:fixed-threshold-green}
Fix a compact $K\subset(0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)$, put
$D=(1+\omega)/(2\omega)$ and $g_0=1/[2(1+\omega)]$, and let
$p_n=\lambda_1^+$.  The following estimates hold uniformly for
$(\omega,\omega_0)\in K$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every $M<\infty$ there is $C_M$, independent of $A$ and $n$,
  with the following property: for every fixed $A\ge1$ and all
  $n\ge N(K,M,A)$, whenever $\lambda=\zeta/n^2$, $|\zeta|\le M$, and
  \[
    \Re\lambda\ge\Re p_n-\pi^2D n^{-2},
    \qquad |\lambda-p_n|\ge A n^{-3},
  \]
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-bounded-threshold-bound}
   |G_n(\lambda)-g_0|\le \frac{C_M}{A}+\frac{C_M}{n}.
  \end{equation}
\item For every fixed $C>0$, if $\lambda_n\to0$ satisfies
  $-C n^{-2}\le\Re\lambda_n\le0$ and
  $n^2|\lambda_n|\to\infty$, then
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-unbounded-threshold-limit}
    G_n(\lambda_n)=g_0+o(1).
  \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use the exact identities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-exact-x-identities}
 x-1=\frac{\lambda(\lambda+2\omega)}{2a},
 \qquad
 \lambda+1-x=\frac{\lambda(\lambda+2)}{2a}.
\end{equation}
For $\lambda=\zeta/n^2$, choose either normalized local branch of
$w_n=n\operatorname{arccosh}x$ satisfying $w_n/n\to0$; the two such
branches differ by sign.  The products used below are invariant under
that sign change.  Taylor's theorem on $|\zeta|\le M$ and
\eqref{eq:fixed-exact-x-identities} give
\begin{align}
 w_n^2&=\frac{\zeta}{D}+O_{K,M}(n^{-2}),\label{eq:fixed-threshold-w}\\
 \frac{\lambda+1-x}{2a\sinh(w_n/n)}
  &=\frac{w_n}{4\omega(1+\omega)n}
    +O_{K,M}(|w_n|n^{-3}).
  \label{eq:fixed-threshold-coefficient}
\end{align}
Here the quotients at $w_n=0$ mean their analytic continuations.  The
first line in particular bounds $|w_n|$ uniformly.  To verify the
second line without dividing by a small $w_n$, divide the two identities
in \eqref{eq:fixed-exact-x-identities} and use
$(\cosh t-1)/\sinh t=\tanh(t/2)$.  This gives the exact factorization
\[
 \frac{\lambda+1-x}{2a\sinh(w_n/n)}
 =\frac{\lambda+2}{2a(\lambda+2\omega)}
   \tanh\!\left(\frac{w_n}{2n}\right).
\]
Uniformly on $K$ and $|\zeta|\le M$, the prefactor on the right is
$1/[2\omega(1+\omega)]+O_{K,M}(n^{-2})$, while Taylor's theorem gives
$\tanh(w_n/(2n))=w_n/(2n)+O_{K,M}(|w_n|^3n^{-3})$.
The uniform bound on $|w_n|$ proves
\eqref{eq:fixed-threshold-coefficient}, including at $w_n=0$.

Since $z^n=e^{-w_n}$, the exact finite Green formula becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-threshold-green-expanded}
 G_n(\zeta/n^2)=g_0+
 \frac{w_n}{4\omega(1+\omega)n}\coth(w_n/2)+E_n,
\end{equation}
where
\[
 |E_n|\le C_{K,M}n^{-2}
       +C_{K,M}n^{-3}|w_n\coth(w_n/2)|.
\]
Let $q_\ell=2\pi\ell/n$ for $1\le\ell\le n/2$.  Uniform Taylor
expansion on the compact $\omega$-projection of $K$ gives
\[
 p^+(q_\ell)=-Dq_\ell^2+O_K(n^{-4})\qquad(\ell=1,2).
\]
In particular,
\begin{align*}
 p^+(q_2)-p_n&=-12\pi^2D n^{-2}+O_K(n^{-4}),\\
 \Re p_n-\pi^2D n^{-2}&=-5\pi^2D n^{-2}+O_K(n^{-4}).
\end{align*}
Hence $p^+(q_2)$ lies strictly to the left of the stated half-plane for
all sufficiently large $n$, uniformly on $K$.  As in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-regular-green}, $p^+(q)$ is a strictly decreasing
function of $q^2$ on a fixed interval $0\le q\le\eta$, while the fast
branch there and both branches for $\eta\le q\le\pi$ stay a fixed
distance to the left of zero.  These facts exclude every remaining
homogeneous pole from the half-plane.  The zero density eigenvalue is
removable in $G_n$, since its $q=0$ summand reduces to
$1/[n(\lambda+2\omega)]$.
The only nonzero pole in the stated scaled half-plane has
$w_n=2\pi\mathrm i$ up to sign and is exactly $\lambda=p_n$.
The derivative of the analytic map $\zeta\mapsto w_n$ at this pole is
bounded above and below away from zero.  The complex inverse-function
theorem, uniformly on the compact parameter set, therefore gives
$|w_n-2\pi\mathrm i|\ge cA/n$ outside
$|\lambda-p_n|<A n^{-3}$.  On a fundamental strip,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coth-distance-bound}
 |\coth(w/2)|\le C\left(1+
       \operatorname{dist}(w,2\pi\mathrm i\Z)^{-1}\right);
\end{equation}
indeed, subtract the nearest $2\pi\mathrm i\ell$, use
$\sinh(w/2)=w/2+O(w^3)$ for $|w|\le1$, and use continuity on the
remaining closed strip together with $\coth(w/2)\to\pm1$ as
$\Re w\to\pm\infty$.  If $|w_n|\le\eta$ for a sufficiently small fixed
$\eta$, then $w_n\coth(w_n/2)$ is bounded by its analytic continuation
at zero.  Thus the correction term in
\eqref{eq:fixed-threshold-green-expanded} is $O_{K,M}(n^{-1})$ and
$E_n=O_{K,M}(n^{-2})$ there.  On the complementary region
$|w_n|>\eta$, the uniform upper bound on $|w_n|$, the exclusion of the
first pole, and \eqref{eq:coth-distance-bound} give
\[
 |\coth(w_n/2)|\le C_{K,M}\left(1+\frac nA\right).
\]
The correction term is consequently bounded by
$C_{K,M}/n+C_{K,M}/A$, and the new remainder bound gives
$|E_n|\le C_{K,M}n^{-2}+C_{K,M}/(An^2)$.
This proves \eqref{eq:fixed-bounded-threshold-bound}, including the
removable point $w_n=0$.

For item 2, first observe that $\lambda_n$ is not a homogeneous pole
for all sufficiently large $n$.  Choose $\eta>0$ as in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-regular-green}.  The fast branch on
$[0,\eta]$ and both branches on $[\eta,\pi]$ stay uniformly away from
zero.  If a remaining pole were equal to $\lambda_n$, it would have
the form $p^+(q_k^*)$; then \eqref{eq:fixed-small-wave-bounds} and
$\Re\lambda_n\ge-Cn^{-2}$ would give $q_k^*=O(n^{-1})$ and hence
$|\lambda_n|=O(n^{-2})$, contrary to
$n^2|\lambda_n|\to\infty$.  The real-part assumptions now imply
$|\Im\lambda_n|/|\lambda_n|\to1$.  By the first identity in
\eqref{eq:fixed-exact-x-identities}, $x-1$ lies, for large $n$, in one
of two closed sectors about the positive or negative imaginary axis.
The expansion
\[
 \operatorname{arccosh}(1+u)=\sqrt{2u}\,(1+O(u))
\]
on those sectors gives
$\Re\operatorname{arccosh}x\ge c\sqrt{|\lambda_n|}$.  Hence
\[
 |z(\lambda_n)|^n
 \le\exp(-cn\sqrt{|\lambda_n|})\longrightarrow0.
\]
Moreover, the two identities in
\eqref{eq:fixed-exact-x-identities} give
$|(\lambda_n+1-x)/s|\le C\sqrt{|\lambda_n|}$.  Substitution in
\eqref{eq:finite-green-z} now proves
\eqref{eq:fixed-unbounded-threshold-limit}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Right-edge root separation]\label{lem:right-edge-separation}
Fix $\omega>0$ and $\omega_0\ge0$, and put
$D=(1+\omega)/(2\omega)$.  There is $N$ such that, for
$n\ge N$, every nonzero eigenvalue other than the protected and affected
roots associated with $q=\pm2\pi/n$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:right-edge-separation}
 \Re\lambda\le \Re\lambda_1^+-\pi^2D n^{-2}.
\end{equation}
The constants can be chosen uniformly when $(\omega,\omega_0)$ ranges
over a compact set with $\omega$ bounded away from zero.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Right-edge window.}
For the uniform assertion, fix a compact
$K\subset(0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)$ containing the given parameter
pair; the pointwise assertion follows from the same argument.  All
parameter-dependent objects below are evaluated at the current pair in
$K$.  Put
\[
 \Omega_n=\left\{\lambda:\Re\lambda\ge
 \Re\lambda_1^+-\pi^2D n^{-2}\right\}.
\]
\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Protected-sector exclusion.}
The protected roots are explicit.  Their slow branch satisfies, uniformly
for small real $q$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slow-dispersion-bound}
 \lambda^+(q)=-Dq^2+O(q^4).
\end{equation}
Choose $q_{\rm loc}>0$ so that the remainder is at most $Dq^2/4$ for
$|q|\le q_{\rm loc}$.  The modes with
$2\pi/n<|q|\le q_{\rm loc}$ are then to the left
of $\Omega_n$, while continuity puts both branches with
$q_{\rm loc}\le|q|\le\pi$ a fixed distance into the left half-plane.  The fast
small-$q$ branch is also separated from zero by a fixed amount.  Thus,
apart from zero, only the protected root associated with
$q=\pm2\pi/n$ lies
in $\Omega_n$ once $n$ is large.  We prove the same exclusion for the
affected sector.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Affected secular equation.}
If $\delta=0$, that sector is homogeneous and the conclusion follows from
\eqref{eq:slow-dispersion-bound}.  Assume $\delta\ne0$ and set
 \begin{equation}\label{eq:threshold-secular-margin}
 f_n(\lambda)=1+2\delta G_n(\lambda),\qquad
 g_0=\frac1{2(1+\omega)},\qquad
 M_0=1+2\delta g_0=\frac{1+\omega_0}{1+\omega}>0.
 \end{equation}
When parameters vary below, $\Omega_n$, $f_n$, $g_0$, and $M_0$ are
evaluated at the current parameter pair.
In a neighborhood of zero, every affected root is a zero of $f_n$.
Indeed, the residue at every slow homogeneous pole is nonzero, so
multiplication by $H_{n,-}^{(0)}$ cancels the pole of $f_n$ but does not
leave a zero there.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Exclusion away from the origin.}
We first exclude, uniformly on $K$, sequences of affected zeros that do
not tend to zero.  Suppose otherwise.  After discarding homogeneous
terms, for which the assertion was already proved, there would be
$n_j\to\infty$, parameters
$(\omega^{(j)},\omega_0^{(j)})\in K$ with
$\delta^{(j)}=\omega_0^{(j)}-\omega^{(j)}\ne0$, and affected zeros
$\lambda_j\in\Omega_{n_j}$, with the window evaluated at that pair,
such that $|\lambda_j|\ge\epsilon>0$.
Gershgorin's theorem places these eigenvalues in a fixed disk and in the
closed left half-plane.  Pass to a subsequence such that
\[
 (\omega^{(j)},\omega_0^{(j)})\longrightarrow(\omega_*,\omega_{0,*}),
 \qquad \lambda_j\longrightarrow\lambda_*\ne0.
\]
Since the boundary of $\Omega_{n_j}$ tends to the imaginary axis,
$\Re\lambda_*=0$.  For a bulk Bloch vector at wave number $q$, the real
part of its quadratic form is the negative of $(1-\cos q)$ times its
squared norm, together with the nonnegative tumbling form.  Equality
therefore forces $q=0$ and equal orientation components, and the
corresponding eigenvalue is zero.  Thus
$\Sigma_{\omega_*}$ meets the imaginary axis only at zero.

Choose a closed disk $U$ about $\lambda_*$ disjoint from zero,
$\Sigma_{\omega_*}$, and the point $a=0$.  By continuity of the bulk
symbol and compactness, after shrinking $U$ and increasing $j$, it is
disjoint from $\Sigma_{\omega^{(j)}}$ and $a=0$ as well.  The local
branches $z(\lambda;\omega^{(j)})$ can then be chosen jointly on $U$ and
satisfy $\sup_U|z(\lambda;\omega^{(j)})|\le\rho<1$.  Equation
\eqref{eq:finite-green-error} gives the uniform estimate
\[
 \sup_{\lambda\in U}
 |G_{n_j}(\lambda;\omega^{(j)})
      -g_\infty(\lambda;\omega^{(j)})|
 \le C_U\frac{\rho^{n_j}}{1-\rho^{n_j}}\longrightarrow0.
\]
For large $j$, the homogeneous odd factor is nonzero on $U$, so the
affected polynomial equation there is the secular equation.  It
therefore passes to
\[
 1+2(\omega_{0,*}-\omega_*)
     g_\infty(\lambda_*;\omega_*)=0.
\]
If $\omega_{0,*}=\omega_*$ this equation is already impossible.
Otherwise it produces an $\ell^2$ eigenvector $u$ of the limiting
infinite defective generator with eigenvalue $\lambda_*$.  This is
impossible because
\begin{equation}\label{eq:infinite-dissipation}
 -\Re\langle u,L_\infty u\rangle
 =\frac12\sum_{j,\sigma}|u_{j+1}^\sigma-u_j^\sigma|^2
   +\sum_j\omega_j|u_j^+-u_j^-|^2.
\end{equation}
Here \eqref{eq:infinite-dissipation} follows by expanding the inner
product, shifting the two hopping sums, and pairing the two tumbling
terms at each site.  These operations are valid first for finitely
supported vectors and then for all $u\in\ell^2$ by density and
boundedness of $L_\infty$.
Equality in \eqref{eq:infinite-dissipation} forces an $\ell^2$ vector to
vanish.  Consequently every sequence of affected zeros in $\Omega_n$
tends to zero, uniformly for parameters in $K$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Scaled exhaustion near the origin.}
It remains to exhaust these affected zeros.  On the
$\zeta=n^2\lambda$ scale, the limiting first pole is
$-4\pi^2D(\omega)$, where $D(\omega)=(1+\omega)/(2\omega)$.  Its range
over the compact $\omega$-projection of
$K$ is compact, and for each $\omega$ it stays uniformly separated from
the corresponding limiting second pole.  Choose a uniform neighborhood
radius smaller than half this separation, and then choose $M_*$ large
enough to contain all the resulting first-pole neighborhoods.  Let
$C_{M_*}$ be the uniform constant
from item 1 of Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-threshold-green}, and put
\[
 \Delta_K=\max_K|\omega_0-\omega|,
 \qquad m_K=\min_K\frac{1+\omega_0}{1+\omega}>0.
\]
Choose $A$ so large that $2\Delta_KC_{M_*}/A<m_K/4$.  Only after these
choices do we increase $n$.
Suppose, to the contrary, that there were a sequence $n\to\infty$,
parameters $(\omega_n,\omega_{0,n})\in K$ with
$\delta_n=\omega_{0,n}-\omega_n\ne0$, and
affected zeros $\lambda_n\in\Omega_n$ outside
$|\lambda-\lambda_1^+|<A n^{-3}$.  Gershgorin's theorem gives
$\Re\lambda_n\le0$, and the preceding paragraph gives $\lambda_n\to0$.
The definition of $\Omega_n$ and \eqref{eq:slow-dispersion-bound} give
$-C n^{-2}\le\Re\lambda_n\le0$.  Pass to a subsequence according as
$\zeta_n=n^2\lambda_n$ is bounded or unbounded.
In the bounded case, pass once more so that the parameters converge and
$\zeta_n\to\zeta_*$.  If $\zeta_*$ is not the corresponding limiting
first pole,
\eqref{eq:fixed-threshold-green-expanded}--\eqref{eq:coth-distance-bound}
give $G_n(\lambda_n)\to g_0$.
If it is the limiting first pole, then $\zeta_n$ lies in its chosen
neighborhood for all sufficiently large $n$, and the first assertion of
that lemma and the choice of $A$ give
$|G_n(\lambda_n)-g_0|\le C_{M_*}/A+o(1)$.  In both alternatives,
$|f_n(\lambda_n)|>m_K/2$ for large $n$, a contradiction.  In the
unbounded case, the second assertion of the lemma gives
$G_n(\lambda_n)=g_0+o(1)$ and the same contradiction.  Hence, for all
sufficiently large $n$, every affected zero in $\Omega_n$ lies in the
first-pole disk, with a threshold uniform on $K$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{First-pole count.}
Finally, on the circle $|\lambda-\lambda_1^+|=A n^{-3}$ write
\[
 G_n(\lambda)=\frac{2R_1}{n(\lambda-\lambda_1^+)}
                 +G_n^{\rm reg}(\lambda).
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-regular-green} and
\eqref{eq:threshold-secular-margin} give
$1+2\delta G_n^{\rm reg}=M_0+O(n^{-1})$.  After multiplication by
$\lambda-\lambda_1^+$, the secular equation becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-first-pole-rouche}
 (\lambda-\lambda_1^+)f_n(\lambda)
 =M_0(\lambda-\lambda_1^+)+\frac{4\delta R_1}{n}
   +O(n^{-1}|\lambda-\lambda_1^+|).
\end{equation}
Here $R_1=O(n^{-2})$.  Enlarge $A$, if necessary, so that the zero of
the first two terms lies strictly inside the circle.  On the circle the
last term is $O(n^{-4})$, whereas the first two terms have modulus
bounded below by $c_A n^{-3}$.  Rouch\'e's theorem therefore counts
exactly one zero in the disk.  It is the affected root of
Lemma~\ref{lem:gap-pole}.  Thus no other affected root occurs in $\Omega_n$,
which proves the assertion.

All estimates above are uniform on the stated compact parameter sets:
$D$, $\delta$, and the generator norms are bounded there, while $M_0$
has a positive minimum.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Gap selection and fixed-rate consequences}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:fixed-gap}]
Taylor expansion of \eqref{eq:hom-gap} gives
\eqref{eq:hom-gap-expansion}.  Lemma~\ref{lem:gap-pole} shows
that, uniformly across $\delta=0$, the affected first root moves toward
zero precisely when $\delta>0$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:right-edge-separation} shows that no other
extended, band-edge, or localized root can compete with this first
pair.  Therefore the protected root has maximal real part when
$\delta\le0$, proving \eqref{eq:slow-defect-gap}, while the affected
root has maximal real part when $\delta>0$.  Substitution of
$q^2=4\pi^2n^{-2}$ in
\eqref{eq:affected-gap-shift} then gives \eqref{eq:fast-defect-gap}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Fixed ratio, vanishing ratio, and a slow-site scaling]
\label{cor:fixed-rate-regimes}
Let the bulk rate $\omega>0$ be fixed.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $\omega_0/\omega\to\rho\in(0,\infty)$, equations
  \eqref{eq:slow-defect-gap}--\eqref{eq:hom-gap-expansion} apply.  For
  $\rho<1$ the protected branch is selected; for
  $\rho>1$ the affected branch and its $n^{-3}$ term are selected.  At
  the boundary $\rho=1$, the protected root is selected whenever
  $\omega_0\le\omega$ and the affected root whenever
  $\omega_0>\omega$, with the expansion uniform across equality.
\item If $\omega_0/\omega\to0$, then \eqref{eq:slow-defect-gap} holds
  for every sufficiently large $n$.
\item In particular, if $n\omega_0\to\beta$ with $\beta\ge0$, then
  \eqref{eq:slow-defect-gap} still holds.  A single slow tumbling site
  therefore has no
  separate $\omega_0\asymp n^{-1}$ gap crossover when the bulk rate is
  fixed.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
In items 2 and 3 one has $\omega_0<\omega$ for every sufficiently large
$n$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:fixed-gap} applies.  In item 1 the convergence
of $\omega_0/\omega$ places the $n$-dependent pairs
$(\omega,\omega_0)$ in one compact parameter set.  The compact-uniform
statement in Theorem~\ref{thm:fixed-gap} therefore permits this
$n$-dependence, and its sign test gives the asserted branch selection,
including crossings through equality.
\end{proof}

\section{Bulk-critical spectral gap}\label{sec:critical-gap}

When $\omega=\alpha/n$ and $\omega_0=\beta/n$, the gap is of order
$n^{-1}$ and the limiting first-mode exceptional scale $\alpha=2\pi$
separates two parity-selection patterns.  Local pole calculations locate
the candidate roots, while the right-edge estimates below exclude every
other root from controlling the gap.

\subsection{Asymptotic regimes and parity selection}

For a real number $x$, write $x_+=\max\{x,0\}$.  The scaled first
wave number is denoted by $q_\star=2\pi$.
For finite $n$, the first homogeneous block is exceptional at
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-critical-ep}
 \alpha_{\mathrm{EP},n}
 =n\sin(q_\star/n)
 =q_\star-\frac{q_\star^3}{6n^2}+O(n^{-4}).
\end{equation}
Thus $\alpha=q_\star$ is the limiting first-mode exceptional scale, not
the exact finite-$n$ coalescence.  At the exact scaling
$\alpha=q_\star$, the residual lattice splitting is of order $n^{-2}$
and is retained in Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-gap}.

\begin{theorem}[Bulk-critical double scaling]\label{thm:critical-gap}
Set
\[
 \omega=\frac{\alpha}{n},\qquad
 \omega_0=\frac{\beta}{n},\qquad
 q_\star=2\pi,
\]
where $\alpha>0$ and $\beta\ge0$ are fixed.
Write $\gamma_n=\gamma_n(\alpha/n,\beta/n)$ for the gap in
Definition~\ref{def:gap}.  Away from $\alpha=q_\star$,
\begin{align}
\alpha<q_\star:\quad
 \gamma_n
 &=\frac{\alpha}{n}
   +\left[\frac{q_\star^2}{2}-2(\alpha-\beta)_+\right]n^{-2}
   +O(n^{-3}),\label{eq:critical-below}\\
\alpha>q_\star:\quad
 \gamma_n
 &=\frac{\alpha-\sqrt{\alpha^2-q_\star^2}}{n}\notag\\
 &\quad+\left[\frac{q_\star^2}{2}
 -2(\beta-\alpha)_+
   \frac{\alpha-\sqrt{\alpha^2-q_\star^2}}
        {\sqrt{\alpha^2-q_\star^2}}\right]n^{-2}
 +O(n^{-3}).\label{eq:critical-above}
\end{align}
At $\alpha=q_\star$,
\begin{align}
 \beta\le q_\star:\quad
 \gamma_n&=\frac{q_\star}{n}
 +q_\star^2\left(\frac12-\frac1{\sqrt3}\right)n^{-2}
 +O(n^{-3}),\label{eq:critical-at-slow}\\
 \beta>q_\star:\quad
 \gamma_n&=\frac{q_\star}{n}
 -2\sqrt{q_\star(\beta-q_\star)}\,n^{-3/2}+O(n^{-2}).
 \label{eq:critical-at-fast}
\end{align}
The constants in the $O(\cdot)$ terms may depend on the fixed pair
$(\alpha,\beta)$.  The side expansions
\eqref{eq:critical-below}--\eqref{eq:critical-above} are for fixed
$\alpha\ne q_\star$ and are not uniform as $\alpha\to q_\star$; the
exact scaling $\alpha=q_\star$ is the separate regime
\eqref{eq:critical-at-slow}--\eqref{eq:critical-at-fast}.  No uniformity
as $\alpha\downarrow0$ is asserted for the remainder constants or the
lower bound on $n$.  The affected parity mode controls the gap for
$\beta<\alpha$ below $q_\star$ and for $\beta>\alpha$ above
$q_\star$; at $\alpha=q_\star$, a faster defect produces the
$n^{-3/2}$ square-root correction.
\end{theorem}

Below $q_\star$, the protected first pair is nonreal and the defect
selects between equal leading real parts through an $n^{-2}$ correction.
Above $q_\star$, the first pair is real and the direction of selection
reverses.  At $\alpha=q_\star$ the two homogeneous roots coalesce on the
leading scale, which accounts for the distinct $n^{-3/2}$ response to a
faster defect.

The proof proceeds in four stages.  Lemma~\ref{lem:critical-green-local}
derives the local Green limits and uniform pole remainders.
Lemmas~\ref{lem:critical-pole-distance} and
\ref{lem:critical-green-off-pole} convert geometric pole separation into
off-pole Green bounds.  Lemmas~\ref{lem:critical-pole-counts}--
\ref{lem:critical-exhaustion} count the pole-neighborhood roots, determine
the zero-wave root, and exclude every remaining root from the spectral
right edge.  The final subsection expands and compares the surviving
first-cluster candidates.

\subsection{Critical Green function and pole neighborhoods}

\begin{definition}[Critical homogeneous poles and residues]
\label{def:critical-poles}
Let $\alpha>0$, set $\omega=\alpha/n$ and $q_\star=2\pi$, and, for an
integer $\ell$, put
\begin{align}
 p_{\ell,n}^{\pm}
 &=\cos(\ell q_\star/n)-1-\frac\alpha n
 \pm\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^2}{n^2}-\sin^2(\ell q_\star/n)},\notag\\
 R_{\ell,n}^\pm
 &=\frac{p_{\ell,n}^\pm+1-\cos(\ell q_\star/n)}
       {p_{\ell,n}^\pm-p_{\ell,n}^\mp}.
 \label{eq:critical-pole-fraction}
\end{align}
The square root is chosen nonnegative when its argument is nonnegative
and with nonnegative imaginary part when its argument is negative.
When the two poles are distinct and $1\le\ell<n/2$, the paired waves
$q=\pm\ell q_\star/n$ contribute
$2R_{\ell,n}^\pm/
\{n(\lambda-p_{\ell,n}^\pm)\}$ for the indicated sign.  The unpaired
endpoint contributions are instead
\[
 \frac{1}{n(\lambda+2\alpha/n)},
 \qquad
 \frac{\varepsilon_n}{n(\lambda+2+2\alpha/n)},
\]
at $q=0$ and $q=\pi$, respectively.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Critical Green limit and pole remainders]
\label{lem:critical-green-local}
Set $\omega=\alpha/n$, where $\alpha>0$ is fixed, and put $q_\star=2\pi$.
For $\nu\in\C$, define the meromorphic function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-green-limit}
 \mathcal G_\alpha(\nu)
 =\frac12\left[1+\frac{\nu}{\kappa}
                   \coth\!\left(\frac\kappa2\right)\right],
 \qquad \kappa^2=\nu(\nu+2\alpha),
\end{equation}
where the right-hand side is independent of the choice of square root
and is interpreted by meromorphic continuation at $\kappa=0$.
The decompositions in items 2--4 are identities of meromorphic
functions.  At each removed pole, the difference defining the regular
part is evaluated by its unique holomorphic extension.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $G_n(\nu/n)\to\mathcal G_\alpha(\nu)$ locally uniformly on every
  compact set containing no pole of \eqref{eq:critical-green-limit}.
\item Fix $\ell\ge1$ with $\alpha\ne\ell q_\star$, and let
  $p_{\ell,n}^\pm$ and $R_{\ell,n}^\pm$ be given by
  \eqref{eq:critical-pole-fraction}.  There are $\eta,C>0$ such
  that
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-simple-regular-bound}
   \left|G_n(\lambda)-
       \frac{2R_{\ell,n}^\pm}
            {n(\lambda-p_{\ell,n}^\pm)}\right|\le C
  \end{equation}
  whenever $|n\lambda-np_{\ell,n}^\pm|\le\eta$, for all sufficiently
  large $n$.
\item If $\alpha=q_\star$, put
  \[
   \lambda_c=\cos(q_\star/n)-1-q_\star/n,
   \qquad r_n=\sqrt{(q_\star/n)^2-\sin^2(q_\star/n)}.
  \]
  There are $\eta,C>0$ and analytic functions $H_n$ on
  $|n(\lambda-\lambda_c)|\le\eta$ such that
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-grouped-green}
   G_n(\lambda)=
   \frac{2(\lambda-\lambda_c-q_\star/n)}
        {n\{(\lambda-\lambda_c)^2-r_n^2\}}+H_n(\lambda),
   \qquad |H_n(\lambda)|\le C.
  \end{equation}
\item There are $\eta,C>0$ and analytic functions $K_n$ on
  $|n\lambda+2\alpha|\le\eta$ such that
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-endpoint-green}
   G_n(\lambda)=\frac{1}{n(\lambda+2\alpha/n)}+K_n(\lambda),
   \qquad |K_n(\lambda)|\le C.
  \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $w_n(\nu)=n\operatorname{arccosh}x(\nu/n)$ on a local branch
satisfying $w_n/n\to0$; such normalized branches differ only by sign.
The exact identities \eqref{eq:fixed-exact-x-identities} give
\begin{align}
 \cosh(w_n/n)
 &=1+\frac{\nu(\nu+2\alpha)}
           {2n^2\{1+(\nu+\alpha)/n\}},
 \label{eq:critical-exact-w}\\
 \frac{\lambda+1-x}{s}
 &=\frac{\nu(2+\nu/n)}
 {2n\{1+(\nu+\alpha)/n\}\sinh(w_n/n)},
 \qquad \lambda=\nu/n.\label{eq:critical-exact-coefficient}
\end{align}
On a compact $\nu$-set, Taylor's theorem applied to
\eqref{eq:critical-exact-w} yields
$w_n^2=\nu(\nu+2\alpha)+O(n^{-1})$ uniformly.  Substitution of
\eqref{eq:critical-exact-coefficient} into the exact formula
\eqref{eq:finite-green-z}, with
$(1+z^n)/(1-z^n)=\coth(w_n/2)$, gives the pointwise limit in item 1.
The resulting expression is even and meromorphic in $w_n$, hence
meromorphic in $w_n^2$, with its value at $w_n=0$ taken by continuation.
On a compact set avoiding the limiting poles, the denominators in this
even expression stay uniformly separated from zero.  Uniform convergence
of $w_n^2$, together with the explicit coefficients in
\eqref{eq:critical-exact-coefficient}, therefore gives uniform convergence
there.  A general branch change has the form
$w_n\mapsto\pm w_n+2\pi\mathrm i n m$; the sign changes in
$\sinh(w_n/n)$ and $\coth(w_n/2)$ cancel, and the additive term is a
period of both factors.  Thus the conclusion agrees on overlapping
branch neighborhoods and is independent of the normalized branch.

For item 2, the limiting poles are
\[
 \nu_\ell^\pm=-\alpha\pm
       \sqrt{\alpha^2-\ell^2q_\star^2}.
\]
The hypothesis $\alpha\ne\ell q_\star$ makes them simple.  Choose a closed
disk $U$ about one of them that contains no other limiting pole.  For
large $n$, $G_n(\nu/n)$ has exactly the corresponding pole
$np_{\ell,n}^\pm$ in $U$, with principal part
$2R_{\ell,n}^\pm/(\nu-np_{\ell,n}^\pm)$.  Item 1 bounds both the Green
function and this principal part on $\partial U$.  Their difference is
removable at the corresponding pole and extends analytically throughout
$U$, so the maximum-modulus theorem bounds it on a smaller concentric
disk.  Replacing $\nu$ by $n\lambda$ gives
\eqref{eq:critical-simple-regular-bound}.

When $\alpha=q_\star$, the two first poles lie at
$\lambda_c\pm r_n$.  Adding their two summands in
\eqref{eq:Gfinite} gives exactly the displayed rational term in
\eqref{eq:critical-grouped-green}.  Choose a fixed disk about $-q_\star$ in
the $\nu$-plane that contains no other limiting pole.  On its boundary,
item 1 bounds $G_n(\nu/n)$ and direct inspection bounds the grouped
term.  Their difference has removable singularities at both first poles
and extends analytically inside.  The maximum-modulus theorem gives the
asserted uniform bound on a smaller disk.

Finally, the first summand of \eqref{eq:Gfinite} is exactly
$1/\{n(\lambda+2\alpha/n)\}$.  The point $-2\alpha$ is distinct from
all the limiting poles with $\ell\ge1$.  Choose a fixed disk about it in
the $\nu$-plane containing no such pole.  For large $n$, the corresponding
scaled disk contains no finite-ring pole except the $q=0$ pole.  On the
boundary, item 1 bounds $G_n(\nu/n)$ and the displayed summand; their
difference has a removable singularity at the endpoint pole and extends
analytically to the disk.  The maximum-modulus theorem on a smaller disk
proves
\eqref{eq:critical-endpoint-green}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Off-pole control}

\begin{lemma}[Critical pole-distance conversion]
\label{lem:critical-pole-distance}
Fix $\alpha>0$ and $R>0$, put $\omega=\alpha/n$ and $q_\star=2\pi$,
and let $w$ satisfy $\cosh(w/n)=x(\lambda)$ in either right-edge
window below.  Define the branch-invariant distances
\[
 d_{0,n}(w)=\operatorname{dist}(w,2\pi\mathrm i n\Z),\qquad
 d_{*,n}(w)=\operatorname{dist}
   \bigl(w,2\pi\mathrm i(\Z\setminus n\Z)\bigr).
\]
Thus $d_{0,n}$ measures distance to the zero-wave lattice points.  In
the right-edge windows below, the nonremovable tumble pole
$\lambda=-2\omega$ lies outside the window, so only the removable
$\lambda=0$ branch is relevant there.  The distance $d_{*,n}$ measures
distance to every nonzero-wave pole.
The following estimates hold for $|\lambda|\le R$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Suppose $\alpha\le q_\star$.  For an integer $L\ge1$, set
  \[
   B=\frac{q_\star^2}{4}\{L^2+(L+1)^2\},\qquad
   \Omega_{B,n}=\{\Re\lambda\ge-\alpha/n-B/n^2\}.
  \]
  Delete disks of radius $An^{-2}$ about the simple homogeneous poles
  in $\Omega_{B,n}$.  When $\alpha=q_\star$, replace the two first-pole
  disks by the disk of radius $An^{-3/2}$ about their midpoint.  There
  are $C=C(\alpha,R)$ and $C_B=C_B(\alpha,B,R)$ such that, for every
  fixed $A\ge1$ and all sufficiently large $n$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-pole-distance-subcritical}
   d_{*,n}(w)^{-1}
   \le C_B\left(1+\frac nA\right)+C\frac n{\sqrt B}
  \end{equation}
  on the remaining part of $\Omega_{B,n}$.
\item Suppose $\alpha>q_\star$.  Put
  \[
   d_1=\alpha-\sqrt{\alpha^2-q_\star^2},\qquad
   d_2=\alpha-\Re\sqrt{\alpha^2-4q_\star^2},
  \]
  fix $d_{\rm cut}\in(d_1,d_2)$, and set
  $\Omega_{{\rm cut},n}=\{\Re\lambda\ge-d_{\rm cut}/n\}$.  After deleting the disk of
  radius $An^{-2}$ about the slow first pole, there is
  $C_{\rm cut}=C_{\rm cut}(\alpha,d_{\rm cut},R)$ such that
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-pole-distance-supercritical}
   d_{*,n}(w)^{-1}\le C_{\rm cut}\left(1+\frac nA\right)
  \end{equation}
  throughout the remaining part of $\Omega_{{\rm cut},n}$, for every fixed
  $A\ge1$ and all sufficiently large $n$.
\end{enumerate}
The thresholds for $n$ may depend on the displayed fixed parameters
and on $A$, but the constants in
\eqref{eq:critical-pole-distance-subcritical}--
\eqref{eq:critical-pole-distance-supercritical} do not depend on
$A$ or $n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Branch reduction.}
A branch change has the form
$w\mapsto\pm w+2\pi\mathrm i n m$.  Both pole sets in the definition
are invariant under this transformation, so both distances are
branch independent.  It is enough to consider $d_{*,n}(w)<1$.
Choose the nearest nonzero-wave representative and use the branch
symmetries to write it as $2\pi\mathrm i\ell$, where
$1\le\ell\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor$, and put
\[
 q=\frac{\ell q_\star}{n},\qquad
 \theta=w-2\pi\mathrm i\ell.
\]
The exact factorization
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-w-pole-factorization}
 2a\{\cosh(w/n)-\cos(\ell q_\star/n)\}
 =D_\ell(\lambda)
 =(\lambda-p_{\ell,n}^+)(\lambda-p_{\ell,n}^-)
\end{equation}
shows that the two zeros over this representative are precisely the
two homogeneous poles of wave number $q$.  Taylor's theorem, $|a|\le
C_R$, and $|\theta|<1$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-cosh-local-bound}
 |D_\ell(\lambda)|
 \le C_R\left(\frac{|\sin q|}{n}|\theta|
                  +\frac{|\theta|^2}{n^2}\right).
\end{equation}

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Subcritical and critical windows.}
Suppose first that $\alpha\le q_\star$.  The poles with indices
$1\le\ell\le L$ lie in $\Omega_{B,n}$ for all sufficiently large
$n$.  Except for $(\alpha,\ell)=(q_\star,1)$, they are simple.  On the
scaled variable $\nu=n\lambda$, the limiting relation is
$w^2=\nu(\nu+2\alpha)$, and at a simple limiting pole
\[
 \frac{dw}{d\nu}=\frac{\nu+\alpha}{w}\ne0.
\]
\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Fixed-index simple poles.}
For each of these fixed indices,
\eqref{eq:critical-cosh-local-bound} first gives
$|D_\ell(\lambda)|\le C_Bn^{-2}$.  The factorization in
\eqref{eq:critical-w-pole-factorization} therefore gives
\[
 \min_{\sigma\in\{+,-\}}
 |\lambda-p_{\ell,n}^{\sigma}|^2
 \le |(\lambda-p_{\ell,n}^+)(\lambda-p_{\ell,n}^-)|
 \le C_Bn^{-2}.
\]
Both poles are $O(n^{-1})$, so $|\lambda|\le C_B'n^{-1}$ and hence
$\nu=n\lambda$ lies in a fixed compact set.
To make the finite-$n$ uniformity explicit, write the local branch as
$w=w_n(\nu)$.  Differentiating the exact relation
$\cosh(w_n/n)=x(\nu/n)$ gives
\[
 \frac{\partial w_n}{\partial\nu}
 =\frac{x'(\nu/n)}{\sinh(w_n/n)}.
\]
The identity
\[
 x(\lambda)-1
 =\frac{\lambda(\lambda+2\omega)}
        {2(1+\omega+\lambda)}
\]
then gives, uniformly on each of these fixed scaled neighborhoods,
\[
 \frac{\partial w_n}{\partial\nu}
 =\frac{\nu+\alpha}{w_n}+O_B(n^{-1}).
\]
After shrinking these finitely many neighborhoods, choose positive
constants $c_B$, $C_B$, and $r_B$, independent of $n$, with the
following property.  For each corresponding finite pole, set
\[
 \nu_{\ell,n}^{\pm}=np_{\ell,n}^{\pm}.
\]
One may choose the local branch so that
$w_n(\nu_{\ell,n}^{\pm})=2\pi\mathrm i\ell$ and
\[
 \left|w_n'(\nu_{\ell,n}^{\pm})\right|\ge 2c_B,
 \qquad
 \sup_{|\nu-\nu_{\ell,n}^{\pm}|\le r_B}|w_n''(\nu)|\le C_B.
\]
The first bound follows from the displayed derivative and the simple
limiting pole.  The second follows from Cauchy's estimate on slightly
larger fixed neighborhoods, using uniform analytic convergence there.
Decrease $r_B$ so that $C_Br_B/2\le c_B$.  Taylor's formula then gives
\[
 |w_n(\nu)-2\pi\mathrm i\ell|
 \ge c_B|\nu-\nu_{\ell,n}^{\pm}|
 \qquad
 (|\nu-\nu_{\ell,n}^{\pm}|\le r_B).
\]
Outside the deleted $An^{-2}$ disk this is at least $c_BA/n$.  On the
complement of those fixed scaled neighborhoods inside the compact
$\nu$-set just obtained, the limiting branch has positive distance from
the corresponding representative $2\pi\mathrm i\ell$.  Uniform
convergence and compactness therefore give a positive lower bound for
the finite-$n$ distance.  The
reciprocal contribution of all these finitely many indices is thus at
most $C_B(1+n/A)$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Coalescing first pair.}
At $\alpha=q_\star$ and $\ell=1$, write
$\Delta=\lambda-\lambda_c$.  The two first poles are
$\lambda_c\pm r_n$, with $r_n=O(n^{-2})$.  Outside the grouped disk,
\[
 |D_1(\lambda)|=|\Delta^2-r_n^2|
 \ge cA^2n^{-3}.
\]
Since $|\sin(q_\star/n)|\asymp n^{-1}$,
\eqref{eq:critical-cosh-local-bound} implies
$|\theta|\ge cA^2/n\ge cA/n$.  This supplies the same reciprocal
bound as a simple pole without separating the coalescing pair.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Remaining wave numbers.}
It remains to treat $\ell\ge L+1$.  Choose
$q_0\in(0,\pi/2)$.  When $q\le q_0$, the two poles are conjugate for
large $n$; write them as
$h_{\ell,n}\pm\mathrm i t_{\ell,n}$.  Every point of
$\Omega_{B,n}$ has horizontal separation at least
\[
 u_{\ell,n}=1-\cos(\ell q_\star/n)-B/n^2
\]
from their common real part.  The midpoint definition of $B$ and
$1-\cos q=q^2/2+O(q^4)$ give
\[
 u_{\ell,n}\ge u_{L+1,n}\ge c\sqrt B\,n^{-2},
 \qquad t_{\ell,n}\ge cq.
\]
For $u\ge0$ and real $y,t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-conjugate-product-bound}
 |(u+\mathrm i(y-t))(u+\mathrm i(y+t))|\ge2u|t|,
\end{equation}
because the difference of the squares of the two sides is
$(u^2+y^2-t^2)^2$.  Hence
$|D_\ell(\lambda)|\ge c u_{\ell,n}q$.  Since
$q\ge2q_\star/n$ and $|\theta|<1$, the right side of
\eqref{eq:critical-cosh-local-bound} is at most
$Cq|\theta|/n$.  Therefore
\[
 |\theta|\ge c n u_{\ell,n}\ge c\sqrt B/n.
\]
When $q\in[q_0,\pi]$, both poles have real part at most
$-c(q_0)<0$, while the boundary of $\Omega_{B,n}$ tends to zero.
Thus $|D_\ell(\lambda)|\ge c(q_0)^2$, which is incompatible with
\eqref{eq:critical-cosh-local-bound} and $|\theta|<1$ for large $n$.
This proves \eqref{eq:critical-pole-distance-subcritical}.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Supercritical window.}
Now suppose $\alpha>q_\star$.  The choice
$d_1<d_{\rm cut}<d_2$ places only the slow first pole in
$\Omega_{{\rm cut},n}$.  Put
\[
 d_{\rm f}=\alpha+\sqrt{\alpha^2-q_\star^2};
\]
the scaled slow and fast first poles converge to $-d_1$ and
$-d_{\rm f}$, respectively, and
$d_{\rm f}>\alpha>d_{\rm cut}$ because $d_2\le\alpha$.  The
simple-pole inverse-function
argument used above gives $|\theta|\ge cA/n$ in a fixed scaled
neighborhood of the slow pole after its disk is deleted.

It remains to justify a uniform bound on the complement of that
neighborhood.  If $|\theta|<1$, then
\eqref{eq:critical-cosh-local-bound} and the factorization
\eqref{eq:critical-w-pole-factorization} give
\[
 \min_{\sigma\in\{+,-\}}
 |\lambda-p_{1,n}^{\sigma}|^2
 \le |D_1(\lambda)|\le Cn^{-2}.
\]
Since $p_{1,n}^{\pm}=O(n^{-1})$, the scaled variable
$\nu=n\lambda$ therefore lies in a fixed disk $|\nu|\le M$.  Fix a
sufficiently small $r>0$ and consider the compact set
\[
 K_r=\{\nu:\Re\nu\ge-d_{\rm cut},\ |\nu|\le M,
                    \ |\nu+d_1|\ge r\}.
\]
There is a positive lower bound for $|\theta|$ over the corresponding
finite-$n$ points.  Otherwise, along a subsequence one would have
$\theta\to0$ and $\nu\to\nu_\infty\in K_r$.  Multiplying
\eqref{eq:critical-cosh-local-bound} by $n^2$ gives
$n^2D_1(\nu/n)\to0$, whereas the scaled factorization gives, uniformly
on this compact set,
\[
 n^2D_1(\nu/n)\longrightarrow
   (\nu_\infty+d_1)(\nu_\infty+d_{\rm f}).
\]
Hence $\nu_\infty=-d_1$ or $-d_{\rm f}$.  The first alternative is
excluded by the definition of $K_r$, and the second by
$d_{\rm f}>d_{\rm cut}$.  Compactness therefore yields
$|\theta|\ge c$ on this complement, uniformly for all sufficiently
large $n$.

Choose $L_0$ with $L_0q_\star>2\alpha$.  For each fixed
$2\le\ell\le L_0$, both pole factors have horizontal distance at
least $c/n$ from $\Omega_{{\rm cut},n}$; hence
$|D_\ell(\lambda)|\ge cn^{-2}$.  Equation
\eqref{eq:critical-cosh-local-bound} gives
$|D_\ell(\lambda)|\le C|\theta|n^{-2}$ when $|\theta|<1$, so
$|\theta|\ge c$.  This also covers a coalescence
$\alpha=\ell q_\star$.

For $\ell>L_0$ and $q\le q_0$, the poles are conjugate,
$t_{\ell,n}\ge cq$, and their horizontal separation from the window
is at least
\[
 u_{\ell,n}=\frac{\alpha-d_{\rm cut}}{n}+1-\cos q.
\]
Equations \eqref{eq:critical-conjugate-product-bound} and
\eqref{eq:critical-cosh-local-bound} give
\[
 |\theta|\ge c n u_{\ell,n}\ge c(\alpha-d_{\rm cut}).
\]
The fixed separation for $q\in[q_0,\pi]$ again rules out
$|\theta|<1$.  Combining the slow-pole estimate with these fixed
lower bounds proves
\eqref{eq:critical-pole-distance-supercritical}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Critical off-pole Green estimate]
\label{lem:critical-green-off-pole}
Fix $\alpha>0$ and $R>0$, put $\omega=\alpha/n$ and $q_\star=2\pi$, and
restrict $\lambda$ to $|\lambda|\le R$.  The following estimates hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Suppose $\alpha\le q_\star$.  For an integer $L\ge1$, set
  \[
   B=\frac{q_\star^2}{4}\{L^2+(L+1)^2\},\qquad
   \Omega_{B,n}=\{\Re\lambda\ge-\alpha/n-B/n^2\}.
  \]
  Delete the disks of radius $A n^{-2}$ about all simple homogeneous
  poles in $\Omega_{B,n}$.  If $\alpha=q_\star$, replace the two disks at the
  first poles by the disk of radius $A n^{-3/2}$ about their midpoint.
  There are constants $C=C(\alpha,R)$ and $C_B=C_B(\alpha,B,R)$,
  independent of $A$ and $n$, such that, for every fixed $A\ge1$, the
  remaining set satisfies the following bound whenever
  $n\ge N(\alpha,B,R,A)$:
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-green-exclusion-bound}
   |G_n(\lambda)|\le
      C_B\left(1+\frac nA\right)+C\frac n{\sqrt B}.
  \end{equation}
\item Suppose $\alpha>q_\star$.  Define
  \[
   d_1=\alpha-\sqrt{\alpha^2-q_\star^2},\qquad
   d_2=\alpha-\Re\sqrt{\alpha^2-4q_\star^2},
  \]
  choose $d_{\rm cut}\in(d_1,d_2)$, and put
  $\Omega_{{\rm cut},n}=\{\Re\lambda\ge-d_{\rm cut}/n\}$.  After
  deleting the disk of radius $A n^{-2}$ about the slow first pole, one has
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-green-exclusion-bound-super}
    |G_n(\lambda)|\le C_{\rm cut}\left(1+\frac nA\right)
  \end{equation}
  throughout $\Omega_{{\rm cut},n}\cap\{|\lambda|\le R\}$.  Here
  $C_{\rm cut}=C_{\rm cut}(\alpha,d_{\rm cut},R)$ is independent of
  $A$ and $n$, and, for every
  fixed $A\ge1$, the estimate holds for
  $n\ge N(\alpha,d_{\rm cut},R,A)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $w=n\operatorname{arccosh}x$ and
$\Theta=(\lambda+1-x)/s$.  Besides
\eqref{eq:finite-green-z}, we use the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-theta-square}
 \Theta^2=
 \frac{\lambda(\lambda+2)}
 { (\lambda+2\omega)(\lambda+2+2\omega)}.
\end{equation}
It follows by squaring the second identity in
\eqref{eq:fixed-exact-x-identities} and using
\[
 x+1=\frac{(\lambda+2)(\lambda+2+2\omega)}{2a}.
\]
A branch change replaces $w$ by
$\pm w+2\pi\mathrm i n m$.  Under the sign change both
$s=\sinh(w/n)$ and $\coth(w/2)$ change sign, while the additive term is
a period.  Thus $\Theta\coth(w/2)$ is unchanged, as are the distances
in Lemma~\ref{lem:critical-pole-distance}.

The right-edge windows stay a fixed multiple of $n^{-1}$ to the right
of the tumble pole $-2\omega$ and a fixed distance to the right of
$-2-2\omega$.  Consequently $|a|$ is bounded below and
\eqref{eq:critical-theta-square} gives $|\Theta|\le C$ there.  Fix a
small $\eta>0$.  If $d_{0,n}(w)\le\eta$, a branch change puts
$|w|\le\eta$.  Taking the quotient of the two identities in
\eqref{eq:fixed-exact-x-identities} where it is defined, extending
through removable points by analytic continuation, and using
$(\cosh t-1)/\sinh t=\tanh(t/2)$ gives the exact factorization
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-zero-exact-factorization}
 \Theta\coth(w/2)
 =\frac{\lambda+2}{\lambda+2\omega}
   \tanh\!\left(\frac{w}{2n}\right)\coth(w/2).
\end{equation}
In the subcritical window,
\[
 \Re(\lambda+2\omega)
 \ge \frac{\alpha}{n}-\frac{B}{n^2}
 \ge \frac{\alpha}{2n}
\]
for all sufficiently large $n$.  In the supercritical window,
\[
 \Re(\lambda+2\omega)\ge\frac{2\alpha-d_{\rm cut}}{n},
\]
where $2\alpha-d_{\rm cut}>0$ because
$d_{\rm cut}<d_2\le\alpha$.  Since also
$|\lambda+2\omega|\ge|\Im\lambda|$, the two cases give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-tumble-distance}
 |\lambda+2\omega|\ge c(n^{-1}+|\Im\lambda|).
\end{equation}
Since $|w|\le\eta$,
the first identity in \eqref{eq:fixed-exact-x-identities} and the upper
bound on $|a|$ imply
$|\lambda(\lambda+2\omega)|\le Cn^{-2}$, and hence
$|\lambda|\le Cn^{-1}$.  Indeed, if
$|\lambda|\le4\omega=4\alpha/n$, this is immediate; otherwise
$|\lambda+2\omega|\ge|\lambda|-2\omega\ge|\lambda|/2$, and therefore
$|\lambda|^2\le2Cn^{-2}$.  The lower bound on $|a|$ and the same identity
then give
\[
 |w|^2\le Cn^2|\lambda|\,|\lambda+2\omega|
       \le Cn|\lambda+2\omega|.
\]
Finally, Taylor's theorem gives, uniformly for $n\ge1$ and
$|w|\le\eta$,
\[
 n\tanh\!\left(\frac{w}{2n}\right)\coth(w/2)
 =1+\mathcal E_n(w),
 \qquad |\mathcal E_n(w)|\le C|w|^2.
\]
Moreover, the preceding bound on $|w|^2$ gives
\[
 \left|
  \frac{\lambda+2}{n(\lambda+2\omega)}\mathcal E_n(w)
 \right|
 \le \frac{C_R|w|^2}{n|\lambda+2\omega|}
 \le C_R.
\]
Substitution in \eqref{eq:critical-zero-exact-factorization} therefore
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-zero-cancellation}
 \Theta\coth(w/2)=
   \frac{\lambda+2}{n(\lambda+2\omega)}+O(1),
\end{equation}
uniformly in either window.  Equation
\eqref{eq:critical-tumble-distance} shows that the leading term, and
hence the entire expression, is bounded.

If $d_{0,n}(w)>\eta$, then
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(w,2\pi\mathrm i\Z)
 =\min\{d_{0,n}(w),d_{*,n}(w)\}.
\]
The finite Green formula and \eqref{eq:coth-distance-bound} therefore
give, throughout this complementary region,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-green-distance-reduction}
 |G_n(\lambda)|\le C\left\{1+
       d_{*,n}(w)^{-1}\right\}.
\end{equation}
Equation \eqref{eq:critical-zero-cancellation} bounds the first region.
On the second region, combine
\eqref{eq:critical-green-distance-reduction} with item 1 or item 2 of
Lemma~\ref{lem:critical-pole-distance}.  This gives respectively
\eqref{eq:critical-green-exclusion-bound} and
\eqref{eq:critical-green-exclusion-bound-super}, with exactly the
stated dependence of the constants.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Root counts and right-edge exhaustion}

\begin{lemma}[Critical pole-neighborhood root counts]
\label{lem:critical-pole-counts}
Set $\omega=\alpha/n$ and $\omega_0=\beta/n$, where
$\alpha>0$ and $\beta\ge0$ are fixed, put $q_\star=2\pi$, and write
$b=\beta-\alpha$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Fix $\ell\ge1$ with $\alpha\ne\ell q_\star$.  There is
  $A_{\ell}>0$ such that, for each sign and every fixed
  $A\ge A_{\ell}$, all sufficiently large $n$ have the property that the disk
  $|\lambda-p_{\ell,n}^{\pm}|<An^{-2}$ contains exactly one zero
  $\widehat p_{\ell,n}^{\pm}$ of $Q_{n,-}$, counted with algebraic
  multiplicity, and
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-uniform-simple-shift}
   \widehat p_{\ell,n}^\pm
   =p_{\ell,n}^\pm
    -4bR_{\ell,n}^\pm n^{-2}+O(n^{-3}).
  \end{equation}
  The threshold in $n$ may depend on $A$.
\item If $\alpha=q_\star$, put
  \[
   \lambda_c=\cos(q_\star/n)-1-q_\star/n,
   \qquad
   r_n=\sqrt{(q_\star/n)^2-\sin^2(q_\star/n)}.
  \]
  There is $A_*>0$ such that, for every fixed $A\ge A_*$ and all
  sufficiently large $n$, the disk
  $|\lambda-\lambda_c|<An^{-3/2}$ contains exactly two zeros of
  $Q_{n,-}$, counted with algebraic multiplicity.  The same two zeros
  occur for every such fixed enlargement $A$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $b=0$, then $Q_{n,-}=H_{n,-}^{(0)}$.  A simple-pole disk contains
exactly its homogeneous root, so item 1 holds with zero shift.  At
$\alpha=q_\star$, the two first homogeneous roots are
$\lambda_c\pm r_n$, where $r_n=O(n^{-2})$; both lie in every fixed
$An^{-3/2}$ disk for all sufficiently large $n$, while all other
homogeneous roots are separated on the $n^{-1}$ scale.  Thus item 2
also holds.  Assume below that $b\ne0$, so $\delta=b/n$.

At a simple pole, Lemma~\ref{lem:critical-green-local} gives
\[
 G_n(\lambda)=\frac{2R_{\ell,n}^\pm}
  {n(\lambda-p_{\ell,n}^\pm)}+G_{\ell,n}^{\rm reg}(\lambda),
 \qquad G_{\ell,n}^{\rm reg}=O(1)
\]
on a fixed scaled neighborhood.  Multiplication of the secular
equation by $\lambda-p_{\ell,n}^\pm$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-simple-rouche}
 (\lambda-p_{\ell,n}^\pm)(1+2\delta G_n)
 =(\lambda-p_{\ell,n}^\pm)
   \left(1+\frac{2b}{n}G_{\ell,n}^{\rm reg}\right)
   +\frac{4bR_{\ell,n}^\pm}{n^2}.
\end{equation}
Compare this expression with
\[
 h_0(\lambda)=\lambda-p_{\ell,n}^\pm
                  +4bR_{\ell,n}^\pm n^{-2}.
\]
If $\sigma_\ell=\sqrt{\alpha^2-\ell^2q_\star^2}$ with the branch inherited
from Definition~\ref{def:critical-poles}, then
\[
 R_{\ell,n}^\pm\longrightarrow
   \frac12\mp\frac{\alpha}{2\sigma_\ell}\ne0;
\]
the inequality follows from $\ell\ge1$.  Thus the residues remain
bounded and bounded away from zero.  Choose $A_\ell$ so that the zero
of $h_0$ lies a fixed relative
distance inside the disk.  On its boundary, the difference from $h_0$
is $O(n^{-3})$, whereas $|h_0|\ge c_A n^{-2}$.  Rouch\'e's theorem
gives exactly one pole-cleared zero.  At that zero,
\eqref{eq:critical-simple-rouche} first gives
$\lambda-p_{\ell,n}^\pm=O(n^{-2})$ and then, after substitution in its
regular-part term, gives the expansion
\eqref{eq:critical-uniform-simple-shift}.

On this disk write
$H_{n,-}^{(0)}=(\lambda-p_{\ell,n}^\pm)J_{\ell,n}$, where
$J_{\ell,n}$ is nonvanishing.  The residue
$R_{\ell,n}^\pm$ is nonzero, and the right side of
\eqref{eq:critical-simple-rouche} is nonzero at the homogeneous pole.
Thus that pole is canceled rather than retained, and the Rouch\'e zero
is exactly one zero of $Q_{n,-}$ with its algebraic multiplicity.  This
proves item 1.

Now let $\alpha=q_\star$ and put $\Delta=\lambda-\lambda_c$.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:critical-green-local},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-grouped-pole-cleared}
 (\Delta^2-r_n^2)(1+2\delta G_n)
 = (\Delta^2-r_n^2)\left(1+\frac{2b}{n}H_n\right)
   +\frac{4b}{n^2}(\Delta-q_\star/n),
\end{equation}
where $H_n=O(1)$ throughout the grouped disk.  Compare this with
\[
 P_{0,n}(\Delta)=\Delta^2-r_n^2
                 +\frac{4b}{n^2}(\Delta-q_\star/n).
\]
With $\Delta=n^{-3/2}Y$, one has locally uniformly in $Y$
\[
 n^3P_{0,n}(n^{-3/2}Y)=Y^2-4bq_\star+O(n^{-1/2}).
\]
Choose $A_*>2\sqrt{|b|q_\star}$.  Rouch\'e's theorem applied to this scaled
quadratic shows that both zeros of $P_{0,n}$ lie a fixed relative
distance inside $|\Delta|<A_*n^{-3/2}$.  On the boundary of every fixed
enlargement, the difference in
\eqref{eq:critical-grouped-pole-cleared} is $O(A^2n^{-4})$, whereas
$|P_{0,n}|\ge c_A n^{-3}$.  Rouch\'e's theorem gives exactly two
pole-cleared zeros.  At the homogeneous poles $\Delta=\pm r_n$, the
right side of \eqref{eq:critical-grouped-pole-cleared} equals
\[
 \frac{4b}{n^2}(\pm r_n-q_\star/n),
\]
which is nonzero for all sufficiently large $n$, because
$r_n=q_\star^2/(\sqrt3\,n^2)+O(n^{-4})$.  The factor
$\Delta^2-r_n^2$ is the complete homogeneous factor on the disk and
the remaining factor of $H_{n,-}^{(0)}$ is nonvanishing.  Hence the two
Rouch\'e zeros are exactly the two zeros of $Q_{n,-}$ there.  Applying
the count once at $A_*$ and once at any fixed $A\ge A_*$ shows that the
annular difference contains no zero, proving item 2.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Critical zero-wave affected root]
\label{lem:critical-endpoint-root}
Set $\omega=\alpha/n$ and $\omega_0=\beta/n$, where
$\alpha>0$ and $\beta\ge0$ are fixed, and put $b=\beta-\alpha$.
For all sufficiently large $n$, the affected polynomial $Q_{n,-}$ has
exactly one zero in a fixed $O(n^{-3})$ neighborhood of
$-2\alpha/n-2b/n^2$.  Counted with algebraic multiplicity, it is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-endpoint-root}
 \lambda_{0,-}=-\frac{2\alpha}{n}-\frac{2b}{n^2}+O(n^{-3}).
\end{equation}
If $b\ne0$, the homogeneous pole $-2\alpha/n$ is canceled; if $b=0$,
the displayed zero is that homogeneous endpoint root.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $\varepsilon=\lambda+2\alpha/n$.  Equation
\eqref{eq:critical-endpoint-green} turns the pole-cleared secular
equation into
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-endpoint-root-equation}
 \varepsilon\left(1+\frac{2b}{n}K_n(\lambda)\right)
       +\frac{2b}{n^2}=0.
\end{equation}
Let $C_0$ be the bound for $K_n$.  On
$|\varepsilon+2b/n^2|=Rn^{-3}$, the difference between the left side
and $\varepsilon+2b/n^2$ is at most
\[
 \frac{2|b|C_0}{n}\left(\frac{2|b|}{n^2}+\frac{R}{n^3}\right)
 \le C_1n^{-3}
\]
for $n\ge R$, where $C_1$ is independent of $R$.  Choose $R>C_1$ and
apply Rouch\'e's theorem to obtain the unique zero and
\eqref{eq:critical-endpoint-root}.

On the endpoint disk write
$H_{n,-}^{(0)}(\lambda)=\varepsilon J_n(\lambda)$, with $J_n$
holomorphic and nonvanishing.  Since
$G_n=1/(n\varepsilon)+K_n$ and $\delta=b/n$,
\[
 Q_{n,-}(\lambda)=J_n(\lambda)
 \left[\varepsilon\left(1+\frac{2b}{n}K_n(\lambda)\right)
       +\frac{2b}{n^2}\right].
\]
For $b\ne0$ the bracket is nonzero at $\varepsilon=0$, so the
homogeneous pole is canceled.  For $b=0$ the bracket is
$\varepsilon$, so the endpoint root is retained.  In either case the
counted zero is the unique zero of $Q_{n,-}$ in the disk.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[First-pole candidate multiset]
\label{def:critical-first-candidates}
Fix $\alpha>0$ and $\beta\ge0$, set $\omega=\alpha/n$,
$\omega_0=\beta/n$, and put $q_\star=2\pi$.  For a fixed $A>0$, let
$\mathcal C_{1,n}(A)$ contain, with algebraic multiplicity, the protected
roots $p_{1,n}^\pm$ and, if $\alpha\ne q_\star$, the affected roots in the two
disks $|\lambda-p_{1,n}^\pm|<A n^{-2}$.  If $\alpha=q_\star$, instead include
all affected roots in the disk centered at
$\lambda_c=\cos(q_\star/n)-1-q_\star/n$ given by
\[
 |\lambda-\lambda_c|<A n^{-3/2}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Critical right-edge exhaustion]
\label{lem:critical-exhaustion}
Set $\omega=\alpha/n$ and $\omega_0=\beta/n$, where
$\alpha>0$ and $\beta\ge0$ are fixed, and put $q_\star=2\pi$.  There is
$A_0=A_0(\alpha,\beta)>0$ such that, for every fixed $A\ge A_0$,
$\mathcal C_{1,n}(A)=\mathcal C_{1,n}(A_0)$ for all sufficiently large
$n$.  Every nonzero eigenvalue with maximal real part belongs to
$\mathcal C_{1,n}(A_0)$.  More precisely, if
$\Lambda_{1,n}$ is the largest real part within that multiset, then every
nonzero eigenvalue outside it
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-exhaustion-separation}
 \Re\lambda\le\Lambda_{1,n}-
 \begin{cases}
  c n^{-2},&\alpha\le q_\star,\\
  c n^{-1},&\alpha>q_\star,
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
for a constant $c=c(\alpha,\beta)>0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Right-edge secular exclusion.}
The protected roots are the homogeneous roots from
Definition~\ref{def:critical-poles}.
Put $b=\beta-\alpha$.  The argument below includes $b=0$; in that case
the affected roots coincide with their homogeneous odd-sector
counterparts, and Lemmas~\ref{lem:critical-pole-counts} and
\ref{lem:critical-endpoint-root} give the same local counts with zero
shift.

Gershgorin's theorem confines all eigenvalues to a fixed disk; choose
$R$ so that this disk lies in $|\lambda|\le R$ and use
Lemma~\ref{lem:critical-green-off-pole} with that $R$.  The constants are
chosen in the following order.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $\alpha\le q_\star$, choose a midpoint value $B$ first so that the
  protected first root lies inside $\Omega_{B,n}$ with an
  $n^{-2}$-scale margin and then so that
  $2|b|C/\sqrt B<1/8$.  If $\alpha>q_\star$, fix once and for all
  $d_{\rm cut}\in(d_1,d_2)$ and use $\Omega_{{\rm cut},n}$.
\item Choose $A_0$ so that
  $2|b|C_B/A_0<1/8$ in the first case or
  $2|b|C_{\rm cut}/A_0<1/4$ in the second.  Increase $A_0$, if necessary, so
  that it exceeds the finitely many simple-pole thresholds and, when
  $\alpha=q_\star$, the grouped threshold in
  Lemma~\ref{lem:critical-pole-counts}.  When $\alpha>q_\star$, include the
  threshold for the fast first pole as well.
\item Fix any $A\ge A_0$.  Only now choose $n$ large enough for the
  estimates with this $A$ and, additionally, for
  $2|b|C_B/n<1/4$ or $2|b|C_{\rm cut}/n<1/4$, respectively.
\end{enumerate}
Since $2\delta=2b/n$, item 1 of the off-pole estimate gives explicitly
\[
 |2\delta G_n|
 \le \frac{2|b|C_B}{n}+\frac{2|b|C_B}{A}
       +\frac{2|b|C}{\sqrt B}<\frac12
\]
when $\alpha\le q_\star$.  Item 2 gives
\[
 |2\delta G_n|
 \le \frac{2|b|C_{\rm cut}}{n}+\frac{2|b|C_{\rm cut}}{A}<\frac12
\]
when $\alpha>q_\star$.  Thus in either case
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-secular-exclusion}
 |2\delta G_n(\lambda)|<\frac12.
\end{equation}
Thus every affected zero in the chosen right-edge window lies in one of
the pole neighborhoods specified in that lemma.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Local counts and candidate stabilization.}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:critical-pole-counts} to the finitely many simple
pole neighborhoods in the chosen window.  Each contains exactly one
affected root, with expansion
\eqref{eq:critical-uniform-simple-shift}.  When $\alpha=q_\star$, apply its
grouped assertion to the first neighborhood; together with
\eqref{eq:critical-secular-exclusion}, these counts account for every
affected root in the window.  When $\alpha>q_\star$, the window contains only
the slow first pole, whereas
Definition~\ref{def:critical-first-candidates}
also contains the fast first disk.  The simple-pole assertion of the
same lemma supplies its unique affected root.

For $\alpha\ne q_\star$, applying the simple-pole count once with $A_0$ and
once with $A$ shows that the annular difference between the two first
disks contains no affected root.  For $\alpha=q_\star$, the corresponding
statement is part of the grouped count.  The protected roots do not
depend on $A$, and hence
$\mathcal C_{1,n}(A)=\mathcal C_{1,n}(A_0)$ with algebraic
multiplicity.  This proves the asserted stabilization.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Subcritical case.}
Whenever $\ell\ge1$ is fixed and $\alpha<\ell q_\star$, the two poles are
nonreal conjugates and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-residue-real-half}
 \Re R_{\ell,n}^\pm=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \Re p_{\ell,n}^\pm
 =-\frac\alpha n-\frac{\ell^2q_\star^2}{2n^2}+O(n^{-4}).
\end{equation}
Suppose first that $\alpha<q_\star$.  Then equations
\eqref{eq:critical-uniform-simple-shift}--
\eqref{eq:critical-residue-real-half} show that the
protected-versus-affected real-part comparison is the same for every fixed $\ell$, and
that the $\ell=2$ cluster is to the left of the first by
$3q_\star^2/(2n^2)+O(n^{-3})$.  We now make the global
partition explicit.  The midpoint definition of $B$ in
Lemma~\ref{lem:critical-pole-distance} puts precisely the fixed-index
nonzero-wave poles with $1\le\ell\le L$ in $\Omega_{B,n}$ for all large
$n$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:critical-pole-counts} gives one affected root at
each of their simple poles, while \eqref{eq:critical-secular-exclusion}
leaves
no affected root on the complement of those disks.  Among the finitely
many clusters with $2\le\ell\le L$, the preceding comparison puts every
selected root at least $c_1n^{-2}$ to the left of the selected $\ell=1$
cluster.  Every remaining nonzero-wave root is outside $\Omega_{B,n}$;
because the protected first root lies inside that window with the margin
fixed in item 1 of the constant choice, such a root is at least
$c_2n^{-2}$ to the left of the largest real part in the $\ell=1$
cluster.  Thus the nonzero-wave roots outside the $\ell=1$ cluster satisfy
\eqref{eq:critical-exhaustion-separation} with
$c=\min(c_1,c_2)$.

For every fixed $\alpha>0$, the zero-wave endpoint is the only unpaired
affected pole on the $O(n^{-1})$ scale; the $q=\pi$ endpoint, when
present, stays a fixed distance into the left half-plane.
Lemma~\ref{lem:critical-endpoint-root} gives its unique affected root and
places its real part at $-2\alpha/n+O(n^{-2})$.  For $\alpha<q_\star$ this is
a distance of order $n^{-1}$ to the left of the $\ell=1$ cluster.  Combined
with the nonzero-wave partition above, this proves
\eqref{eq:critical-exhaustion-separation} for $\alpha<q_\star$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Critical case.}
At $\alpha=q_\star$, the first protected cluster contains
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-protected-reference}
 -\frac{q_\star}{n}
 +\left(-\frac{q_\star^2}{2}+\frac{q_\star^2}{\sqrt3}\right)n^{-2}
 +O(n^{-3}).
\end{equation}
For every fixed $\ell\ge2$, equations
\eqref{eq:critical-uniform-simple-shift}--
\eqref{eq:critical-residue-real-half} apply with $\alpha=q_\star$.  If
$\beta\le q_\star$, its largest possible real part is below
\eqref{eq:critical-protected-reference} by
\[
 \left[\frac{(\ell^2-1)q_\star^2}{2}
       +\frac{q_\star^2}{\sqrt3}-2(q_\star-\beta)\right]n^{-2}
 +O(n^{-3}),
\]
which is positive because $q_\star>2\sqrt3$.  If $\beta>q_\star$, the affected
higher modes move to the left and the protected comparison is even
stronger.  Estimate \eqref{eq:critical-secular-exclusion} accounts for
all remaining affected roots.  The endpoint formula
\eqref{eq:critical-endpoint-root} from
Lemma~\ref{lem:critical-endpoint-root} puts the zero-wave affected root
at $-2q_\star/n+O(n^{-2})$ and
hence a distance of order $n^{-1}$ to the left of the $\ell=1$ cluster.
This proves the assertion at $\alpha=q_\star$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Supercritical case.}
Finally, if $\alpha>q_\star$, the first slow pole has decay coefficient
$d_1=\alpha-\sqrt{\alpha^2-q_\star^2}$.  By the choice of
$d_{\rm cut}$, the only protected pole in $\Omega_{{\rm cut},n}$ is the
first slow pole, and
\eqref{eq:critical-secular-exclusion}--
\eqref{eq:critical-uniform-simple-shift} show that its affected partner
is the only affected root there.  Both have real part
$-d_1/n+O(n^{-2})$, whereas every nonzero root outside the window has
real part at most $-d_{\rm cut}/n$.  In particular, the endpoint root
\eqref{eq:critical-endpoint-root} is at $-2\alpha/n+O(n^{-2})$ and lies
outside the window because $d_{\rm cut}<2\alpha$.  This gives a
separation of order
$n^{-1}$ and completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Critical first cluster]\label{def:critical-first-cluster}
Under the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:critical-exhaustion}, choose
$A_0=A_0(\alpha,\beta)$ as in that lemma.  The stabilized multiset
$\mathcal C_{1,n}(A_0)$, independent of every fixed enlargement
$A\ge A_0$ for all sufficiently large $n$, is the critical first
cluster.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Proof of the bulk-critical theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-gap}]
For $\alpha<q_\star$, the protected first pair has
\[
 \Re\lambda_1^+=-\frac{\alpha}{n}-\frac{q_\star^2}{2n^2}
 +O(n^{-3}).
\]
The pole residue tends to
$R_1=\tfrac12+\ii\alpha/(2\sqrt{q_\star^2-\alpha^2})$, and
$\delta=(\beta-\alpha)/n$.  Hence the affected real part shifts by
$-2(\beta-\alpha)n^{-2}+O(n^{-3})$ by
\eqref{eq:critical-uniform-simple-shift}.  Taking the larger real part
gives \eqref{eq:critical-below}.

For $\alpha>q_\star$, put
$\sigma_\star=\sqrt{\alpha^2-q_\star^2}$.  The protected rate is
$(\alpha-\sigma_\star)n^{-1}+q_\star^2(2n^2)^{-1}+O(n^{-3})$, while
$R_1\to-(\alpha-\sigma_\star)/(2\sigma_\star)$.  The same pole equation
shifts the affected eigenvalue by
$2(\beta-\alpha)(\alpha-\sigma_\star)/(\sigma_\star n^2)+O(n^{-3})$.
Selection of the larger real part gives \eqref{eq:critical-above}.
Lemma~\ref{lem:critical-exhaustion} shows in both cases that no root
outside the critical first cluster of
Definition~\ref{def:critical-first-cluster} can compete with the selected
root.

At $\alpha=q_\star$, the exact lattice splitting of the homogeneous first
block is
\[
 \sqrt{\omega^2-\sin^2(q_\star/n)}
   =\frac{q_\star^2}{\sqrt3\,n^2}+O(n^{-4}),
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:critical-at-slow} when the protected root has
maximal real part.
Near the center of this block, put
\[
 \lambda_c=\cos(q_\star/n)-1-q_\star/n,\qquad
 r_n=\sqrt{(q_\star/n)^2-\sin^2(q_\star/n)},\qquad
 \Delta=\lambda-\lambda_c.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:critical-green-local} gives the uniform identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-green-two-terms}
 G_n(\lambda)
 =\frac{2(\Delta-q_\star/n)}{n(\Delta^2-r_n^2)}+H_n(\lambda),
 \qquad |H_n(\lambda)|\le C,
\end{equation}
throughout a fixed $n\Delta$-neighborhood of zero.  In particular this
bound is uniform on every contour used below.

Put $\tau=n^{-1/2}$ and scale $\Delta=n^{-3/2}Y$.
For $\beta>q_\star$, put $b=\beta-q_\star$.  Since
\[
 n^4r_n^2=\frac{q_\star^4}{3}+O(n^{-2}),
\]
the secular equation, uniformly for $Y$ in a compact set bounded away
from zero, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-fast-scaled-secular}
 F_n(Y)=1-\frac{4q_\star b}{Y^2}+\frac{4b\tau}{Y}
             +O(\tau^2)=0.
\end{equation}
The limiting function has simple zeros
$Y_\pm=\pm2\sqrt{q_\star b}$.  On
$|Y-Y_\pm|=C\tau$, its modulus is bounded below by
$cC\tau$, while the difference in
\eqref{eq:critical-fast-scaled-secular} is at most $C_1\tau$.
Choosing $C>C_1/c$, Rouch\'e's theorem gives one zero in each disk and
hence two affected roots
\[
 \Delta=\mathord\pm2\sqrt{q_\star b}\,n^{-3/2}+O(n^{-2}).
\]
The positive displacement gives \eqref{eq:critical-at-fast}.

For $\beta<q_\star$, put $h=q_\star-\beta>0$.  With the same scaling, the secular
equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-slow-scaled-secular}
 \widetilde F_n(Y)=1+\frac{4q_\star h}{Y^2}
                    -\frac{4h\tau}{Y}+O(\tau^2)=0.
\end{equation}
Its two leading zeros are $Y_\pm=\pm2\ii\sqrt{q_\star h}$.  Substitution shows
that $Y_\pm+2h\tau$ has residual $O(\tau^2)$.  On the circles
$|Y-(Y_\pm+2h\tau)|=C\tau^2$, the affine part has modulus at
least $cC\tau^2$ and the remaining error is at most
$C_1\tau^2$.  A second application of Rouch\'e's theorem therefore
gives the affected pair
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-slow-affected-pair}
 \Delta=\mathord\pm2\ii\sqrt{q_\star h}\,n^{-3/2}
        +2h n^{-2}+O(n^{-5/2}).
\end{equation}
Its decay rate exceeds the protected decay rate by
\[
 \left(\frac{q_\star^2}{\sqrt3}-2h\right)n^{-2}+O(n^{-5/2}).
\]
This coefficient is positive for $0<h\le q_\star$ because
$q_\star=2\pi>2\sqrt3$.  The protected root therefore has maximal real
part for every $\beta<q_\star$; at $\beta=q_\star$ the two parity roots
coincide.  This proves
\eqref{eq:critical-at-slow}; the case $\beta>q_\star$ was obtained from the
positive displacement above.
Lemma~\ref{lem:critical-exhaustion} supplies the required exclusion of every
higher, endpoint, and off-pole affected root in both cases.
\end{proof}

\section{Telegraph comparison}\label{sec:telegraph}

\subsection{Homogeneous continuum approximations}

The standard one-dimensional telegraph system follows from the
right- and left-moving continuum equations in
\cite[Sec.~II, Eqs.~(2.2)--(2.4)]{Schnitzer1993}.  In that convention each
collision retains or reverses direction with equal probability, so a
collision rate $2\omega$ produces an actual reversal rate $\omega$.
The direct run-and-tumble derivation of the telegrapher equation is also
given in \cite[Sec.~2, Eqs.~(1)--(7)]{Angelani2015}.
Setting the speed to one gives the normalization below.  We compare it
with the next lattice correction before introducing an independently
defined continuum point interaction at the same unit site spacing.  No
convergence of lattice operators under a vanishing-spacing limit is claimed.

\begin{definition}[Two continuum approximations]\label{def:telegraph}
Let $\rho$ denote total density and $m$ polarization.  Both fields are
$n$-periodic in $x$.  For a homogeneous ring of circumference $n$, the
standard telegraph system is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:telegraph-system}
 \partial_t\rho=-\partial_xm,
 \qquad
 \partial_tm=-\partial_x\rho-2\omega m.
\end{equation}
Its slow Fourier eigenvalue at wave number $q$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:telegraph-eigenvalue}
 \lambda_{\rm tel}^+(q)=-\omega+\sqrt{\omega^2-q^2}.
\end{equation}
Here and below the square root has nonnegative real part and, when it is
purely imaginary, nonnegative imaginary part.
The lattice-diffusion-corrected system is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corrected-telegraph-system}
 \partial_t\rho=-\partial_xm+\tfrac12\partial_x^2\rho,
 \qquad
 \partial_tm=-\partial_x\rho+\tfrac12\partial_x^2m-2\omega m.
\end{equation}
Its slow eigenvalue is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corrected-telegraph-eigenvalue}
 \lambda_{\rm td}^+(q)=-\frac{q^2}{2}-\omega
                         +\sqrt{\omega^2-q^2}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Second-order lattice expansion]
\label{lem:telegraph-taylor}
Let $\rho,m\in C^4(\mathbb R/n\Z)$ and sample
$u_j^\pm=\{\rho(j)\pm m(j)\}/2$.  The homogeneous lattice generator
induces
\begin{align}
 \dot\rho_j&=-m'(j)+\tfrac12\rho''(j)+r_{\rho,j},\notag\\
 \dot m_j&=-\rho'(j)+\tfrac12m''(j)-2\omega m(j)+r_{m,j},
 \label{eq:lattice-taylor-system}
\end{align}
where
\begin{align}
 |r_{\rho,j}|&\le\tfrac16\|m'''\|_\infty
                    +\tfrac1{24}\|\rho^{(4)}\|_\infty,\notag\\
 |r_{m,j}|&\le\tfrac16\|\rho'''\|_\infty
                    +\tfrac1{24}\|m^{(4)}\|_\infty.
 \label{eq:lattice-taylor-remainder}
\end{align}
Thus \eqref{eq:corrected-telegraph-system} is the second-order
long-wave truncation of the lattice equations, with the remainder
controlled in the sampled $\ell^\infty$ norm by
\eqref{eq:lattice-taylor-remainder}.  For a Fourier mode of wave number
$q$, the centered first-difference error is of order $q^3$ and the
centered second-difference error is of order $q^4$, making the long-wave
meaning explicit.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Adding and subtracting the two homogeneous equations
\eqref{eq:generator-plus}--\eqref{eq:generator-minus} gives the exact
sampled identities
\begin{align*}
 \dot\rho_j
 &=\frac{\rho(j-1)+\rho(j+1)}2-\rho(j)
   +\frac{m(j-1)-m(j+1)}2,\\
 \dot m_j
 &=\frac{\rho(j-1)-\rho(j+1)}2
   +\frac{m(j-1)+m(j+1)}2-m(j)-2\omega m(j).
\end{align*}
Taylor's theorem on the unit intervals adjacent to $j$ bounds the
error in each centered first difference by one sixth of the supremum
of the third derivative, and the error in each centered second
difference by one twenty-fourth of the supremum of the fourth
derivative.  Substitution gives
\eqref{eq:lattice-taylor-system}--\eqref{eq:lattice-taylor-remainder}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Homogeneous lattice versus telegraph spectral decay]
\label{thm:telegraph-comparison}
Fix $\omega>0$, let $q=2\pi/n$, and define
$\gamma_{\rm tel}=-\Re\lambda_{\rm tel}^+(q)$ and
$\gamma_{\rm td}=-\Re\lambda_{\rm td}^+(q)$.  Then, as $n\to\infty$,
\begin{align}
 \gamma_n^{\rm hom}-\gamma_{\rm tel}
 &=\frac{q^2}{2}
   -\left(\frac1{24}+\frac1{6\omega}\right)q^4+O(q^6),
   \label{eq:telegraph-error}\\
 \gamma_n^{\rm hom}-\gamma_{\rm td}
 &=-\left(\frac1{24}+\frac1{6\omega}\right)q^4+O(q^6).
   \label{eq:corrected-telegraph-error}
\end{align}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:telegraph-ratios}
 \frac{\gamma_{\rm tel}}{\gamma_n^{\rm hom}}
 \longrightarrow\frac1{1+\omega},
 \qquad
 \frac{\gamma_{\rm td}}{\gamma_n^{\rm hom}}\longrightarrow1.
\end{equation}
Separately, let $\alpha>0$ be fixed and set $\omega=\alpha/n$.  The
standard telegraph decay rate is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:telegraph-critical}
 \gamma_{\rm tel}
 =\frac{\alpha-\Re\sqrt{\alpha^2-(2\pi)^2}}{n},
\end{equation}
which agrees with the coefficient of $n^{-1}$ in
\eqref{eq:critical-below}--\eqref{eq:critical-at-fast}.  For fixed
$\beta\ge0$, the defect-dependent corrections under
$\omega_0=\beta/n$, of orders $n^{-2}$ and $n^{-3/2}$ in those formulas,
are beyond this homogeneous telegraph comparison.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The exact lattice dispersion replaces $-q^2/2$ by $\cos q-1$ and $q$
inside the square root by $\sin q$.  Expanding
$\cos q$, $\sin^2q$, and the square root gives
\eqref{eq:telegraph-error}--\eqref{eq:corrected-telegraph-error}.
Dividing by the leading exact rate $(1+\omega)q^2/(2\omega)$ gives
\eqref{eq:telegraph-ratios}.  Substitution of $\omega=\alpha/n$ and
$q=2\pi/n$ into \eqref{eq:telegraph-eigenvalue}, followed by taking
the negative real part, gives \eqref{eq:telegraph-critical}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Point-defect telegraph operator}

For the Schr\"odinger operator, a one-dimensional point interaction can be
encoded by continuity at the interaction point and a jump in the derivative;
see \cite[Part~I, Ch.~I.3, Eq.~(3.1.9)]{AlbeverioGesztesyHoeghKrohnHolden2005}.
After elimination of $\rho$ from the eigenvalue equations below, the domain
condition produces an analogous derivative jump for $m$.  The operator here
is nevertheless a first-order kinetic system, not a Schr\"odinger operator or
a vanishing-spacing limit of the lattice generator.

\begin{definition}[Point-defect telegraph operator]
\label{def:telegraph-defect-operator}
Let $\eta\in\mathbb R$.  For the kinetic defect in
Definition~\ref{def:generator}, one has
$\eta=\omega_0-\omega\ge-\omega$.
Here the continuum coordinate uses the same unit site spacing as the
lattice, so the circle has circumference $n$.  The Dirac coupling has
coefficient $2\eta$ and enters the polarization equation as
$-2\eta\,\delta_0(x)m$; no further lattice-spacing limit or rescaling
of $\eta$ is imposed.
On the circle cut at zero, define the point-defect telegraph operator by
\[
 \mathcal A_\eta(\rho,m)=(-m',-\rho'-2\omega m)
 \quad\text{on }(0,n),
\]
with domain consisting of $m,\rho\in H^1(0,n)$ having traces that
satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:telegraph-jump-domain}
 m(0+)=m(n-),\qquad
 \rho(0+)-\rho(n-)=-2\eta m(0).
\end{equation}
This is the distributional realization obtained by adding
$-2\eta\,\delta_0(x)m$ to the second equation in
\eqref{eq:telegraph-system}.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Off-spectrum point-defect telegraph secular equation]
\label{prop:telegraph-defect}
For the operator in Definition~\ref{def:telegraph-defect-operator},
the domain is dense and the operator is closed.  Define the homogeneous
telegraph spectrum
on this circle by
\[
 \Sigma_{\rm tel}(n,\omega)
 =\left\{-\omega\pm
   \sqrt{\omega^2-(2\pi\ell/n)^2}:\ell\in\mathbb Z\right\}.
\]
For $\lambda\notin\Sigma_{\rm tel}(n,\omega)$, put
\[
 \kappa=\sqrt{\lambda(\lambda+2\omega)}
\]
and interpret
$\kappa^{-1}\coth(n\kappa/2)$ as the even meromorphic function of
$\kappa$ obtained by analytic continuation; no sign choice for the
square root is required.  The affected-parity eigenvalues outside
$\Sigma_{\rm tel}(n,\omega)$ are exactly the zeros of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:telegraph-defect}
 1+\eta\frac{\lambda}{\kappa}
            \coth\left(\frac{n\kappa}{2}\right)=0.
\end{equation}
For the involution
\[
 \mathcal R(\rho,m)(x)=(\rho(n-x),-m(n-x)),
\]
the reflection-even telegraph modes are unchanged.  Formula
\eqref{eq:telegraph-defect} is the
continuum analogue of \eqref{eq:resolvent-form} at unit site spacing;
the exact lattice formula retains $1-\cos q$ and $\sin q$, and
\eqref{eq:telegraph-error}--\eqref{eq:corrected-telegraph-error} quantify
the resulting spectral-decay error.
At points of $\Sigma_{\rm tel}(n,\omega)$, this proposition identifies
only the protected reflection-even family; it does not assert an
affected-sector Jordan classification at homogeneous spectral points.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Domain and closedness.}
The jump in \eqref{eq:telegraph-jump-domain} cancels the delta
distribution in $-\rho'-2\eta\delta_0m(0)$, while continuity of $m$
prevents a delta distribution in $-m'$.  Thus the stated domain gives
the point interaction on $L^2((0,n);\mathbb C^2)$.  It is densely
defined because it contains $C_c^\infty((0,n);\mathbb C^2)$, and it is
closed:
the graph norm controls $\|m'\|_2$ directly and controls
$\|\rho'\|_2$ by
$\|\rho'\|_2\le\|(\mathcal A_\eta(\rho,m))_2\|_2+
2\omega\|m\|_2$; the trace theorem then preserves
\eqref{eq:telegraph-jump-domain} under graph-norm limits.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Homogeneous spectrum.}
For $\eta=0$, substitution of the periodic Fourier mode
$e^{2\pi\mathrm i\ell x/n}$ gives
$\lambda(\lambda+2\omega)+(2\pi\ell/n)^2=0$.  Hence its two roots,
as $\ell$ ranges over $\mathbb Z$, are exactly
$\Sigma_{\rm tel}(n,\omega)$.
Indeed, the periodic Fourier transform identifies the homogeneous
operator, with its periodic $H^1$ domain, with the orthogonal direct sum
over $\ell\in\mathbb Z$ of the matrices
\[
 M_\ell:=\begin{pmatrix}
  0&-\mathrm i q_\ell\\
  -\mathrm i q_\ell&-2\omega
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad q_\ell=\frac{2\pi\ell}{n}.
\]
Their characteristic polynomials are
$\lambda(\lambda+2\omega)+q_\ell^2$.  To see that the direct sum adds
no further spectrum, fix $\zeta$ outside this root set.  Its inverse
block is
\[
 (\zeta I-M_\ell)^{-1}
 =\frac{1}{\zeta(\zeta+2\omega)+q_\ell^2}
  \begin{pmatrix}
   \zeta+2\omega&-\mathrm i q_\ell\\
   -\mathrm i q_\ell&\zeta
  \end{pmatrix}.
\]
For all sufficiently large $|\ell|$, the denominator has modulus at
least $q_\ell^2/2$, so
$\|(\zeta I-M_\ell)^{-1}\|\le C_\zeta/(1+|q_\ell|)$; the finitely many
remaining inverse blocks are bounded as well.  Hence their direct sum
maps $L^2$ boundedly into the weighted Fourier domain corresponding to
periodic $H^1$, and is the resolvent of the homogeneous operator.
Thus the displayed roots exhaust its spectrum.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Defect eigenfunctions.}
Let $\lambda\notin\Sigma_{\rm tel}(n,\omega)$.  The
eigenvalue equations away from zero are
\[
 -m'=\lambda\rho,\qquad -\rho'-2\omega m=\lambda m.
\]
Thus $\lambda\ne0$ and elimination of $\rho$ gives
$m''=\kappa^2m$.  Write
\[
 m(x)=A\cosh\!\left(\kappa(x-n/2)\right)
      +B\sinh\!\left(\kappa(x-n/2)\right).
\]
The equality $m(0)=m(n)$ gives $B=0$, since
$\sinh(n\kappa/2)=0$ would put $\lambda$ in the homogeneous spectrum.
Using $\rho=-m'/\lambda$, the second jump condition in
\eqref{eq:telegraph-jump-domain} is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:telegraph-derivative-jump}
 m'(0)-m'(n)=2\eta\lambda m(0).
\end{equation}
Substitution of the even hyperbolic-cosine solution into
\eqref{eq:telegraph-derivative-jump} gives
\[
 -2\kappa\sinh(n\kappa/2)
 =2\eta\lambda\cosh(n\kappa/2),
\]
which is exactly \eqref{eq:telegraph-defect}.
Conversely, if \eqref{eq:telegraph-defect} holds outside the homogeneous
spectrum, take $A=1$, $B=0$ in the displayed hyperbolic solution and
put $\rho=-m'/\lambda$.  Equation
\eqref{eq:telegraph-derivative-jump} and the continuity of $m$ then give
both conditions in \eqref{eq:telegraph-jump-domain}, so this construction
is a nonzero eigenfunction.  Moreover
$m(n-x)=m(x)$ and $\rho(n-x)=-\rho(x)$, hence
$\mathcal R(\rho,m)=-(\rho,m)$ and the eigenfunction has affected parity.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Resolvent form and protected parity.}
Equivalently, the polarization entry of the homogeneous telegraph
resolvent is
\[
 \frac1n\sum_{\ell\in\Z}
 \frac{\lambda}{\lambda(\lambda+2\omega)+(2\pi\ell/n)^2}
 =\frac{\lambda}{2\kappa}\coth\left(\frac{n\kappa}{2}\right).
\]
The series converges normally away from the homogeneous spectrum and
the last expression supplies its meromorphic continuation when
$\kappa$ is purely imaginary, in agreement with the direct jump
calculation.  Finally, a reflection-even vector satisfies
$m(0+)=-m(n-)$; continuity of $m$ therefore gives $m(0)=0$.  The jump
vanishes, so these modes are protected exactly as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:protected-sector}.
\end{proof}

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

The rank-one polarization defect organizes the finite-ring problem into
two complementary parts.  The protected parity sector retains the
homogeneous spectrum, while the affected sector is governed by one
scalar Green function.  This structure produces the derivative,
Chebyshev, transfer, and parity forms of the characteristic polynomial
and reduces the affected Jordan classification to finite polynomial
root orders.  The flat band at $\omega=1$ is the singular exception, with
its tuned $J_3$ block recorded separately.

On the infinite line, the same Green function distinguishes extended
bulk spectrum from defect-localized modes.  The noncompact off-bulk modes
are described by the matching equations and their cubic elimination, and
the singular transfer value supports a compact mode on three sites.  The
transfer alternatives exclude embedded $\ell^2$ modes except at the
singular flat band, whose eigenspace is given explicitly.  Each simple
noncompact localized eigenvalue produces a finite-ring eigenvalue with
exponentially small error and uniformly localized squared right-eigenvector
amplitude.

The gap results separate two physical mechanisms.  At fixed
$\omega>0$, reflection leaves one first-mode decay rate unchanged, so a
slow site does not create a new scaling; a faster site changes only the
$n^{-3}$ correction.  When both rates scale as $n^{-1}$, the gap itself
is of order $n^{-1}$.  The limiting first-mode exceptional scale
$\alpha=2\pi$ then reverses which side of the equal-rate line selects the
affected parity mode and produces the $n^{-3/2}$ boundary correction for
a faster defect.

The continuum comparison identifies why the standard telegraph equation
does not reproduce the fixed-rate lattice decay: unit-rate Poisson
hopping contributes one half of the componentwise Laplacian.  Adding this
term recovers the leading lattice rate, while the separately defined
point-defect telegraph operator reproduces the parity protection and scalar
secular structure at unit site spacing.  Together, the finite, infinite,
asymptotic, and continuum formulations expose the same protected/affected
decomposition across every regime considered here.
